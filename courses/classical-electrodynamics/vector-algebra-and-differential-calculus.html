Vector analysis: from geometry to local field variation

Leyan TANG · Classical Electrodynamics · Griffiths, Introduction to Electrodynamics, Chapter 1, Sections 1.1–1.2

Vector analysis supplies the language used to describe electromagnetic fields. These notes connect two tasks: combining vectors at a point, and describing how a field changes from one point to the next.

Study guide · Sections 1.1–1.2

Geometry first, derivatives second

Three questions connect the material:

  1. What do dot and cross products tell us geometrically?
  2. How do gradient, divergence, and curl describe different kinds of local variation?
  3. How can component calculations check an identity or a sign?

Notation and scope. Bold symbols denote real three-dimensional vectors; A=|A|A=|\mathbf A| denotes a magnitude. The unit vectors x^,y^,z^\hat{\mathbf x},\hat{\mathbf y},\hat{\mathbf z} form a fixed, right-handed orthonormal Cartesian basis. Scalar fields are written f,g,Tf,g,T, and vector fields A,B,v\mathbf A,\mathbf B,\mathbf v. Derivative formulas apply where the fields are sufficiently smooth; quotient formulas additionally require g≠0g\ne0.

1.1 Vector algebra

1.1.1 Vectors, scalars, and the elementary operations

A vector has magnitude and direction. A free vector can be translated without changing the vector itself; this does not mean that location is irrelevant in physics. A vector field assigns a vector to each position. A scalar has no spatial direction, but its value may be positive, negative, or zero and may carry units.

Addition is constructed head to tail. Subtraction adds the opposite vector. Multiplication by a negative scalar reverses direction:

A+B=B+A,(A+B)+C=A+(B+C).\mathbf A+\mathbf B=\mathbf B+\mathbf A,\qquad (\mathbf A+\mathbf B)+\mathbf C=\mathbf A+(\mathbf B+\mathbf C).
A-B=A+(-B),a(A+B)=aA+aB.\mathbf A-\mathbf B=\mathbf A+(-\mathbf B),\qquad a(\mathbf A+\mathbf B)=a\mathbf A+a\mathbf B.

1.1.2 Dot product: projection and angle

For nonzero vectors with angle θ\theta between them,

A·B=ABcosθ.\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=AB\cos\theta.

The result is a scalar: the magnitude of one vector multiplied by the signed projection of the other along it. It is positive for an acute angle, zero for perpendicular vectors, and negative for an obtuse angle. Parallel vectors pointing in opposite directions therefore have dot product -AB-AB, rather than ABAB.

A·B=B·A,A·(B+C)=A·B+A·C.\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf A,\qquad \mathbf A\cdot(\mathbf B+\mathbf C)=\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B+\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf C.

Since A·A=A2\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf A=A^2, the difference C=A-B\mathbf C=\mathbf A-\mathbf B gives a useful geometric check:

C2=(A-B)·(A-B)=A2+B2-2ABcosθ.C^2=(\mathbf A-\mathbf B)\cdot(\mathbf A-\mathbf B)=A^2+B^2-2AB\cos\theta.

1.1.3 Cross product: oriented area

For nonparallel vectors,

A×B=ABsinθn^.\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=AB\sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n}.

The unit normal n^\hat{\mathbf n} is chosen by the right-hand rule, curling from the first vector toward the second. The magnitude is the area of their parallelogram. Reversing the order reverses the normal:

B×A=-A×B,A×(B+C)=A×B+A×C.\mathbf B\times\mathbf A=-\mathbf A\times\mathbf B,\qquad \mathbf A\times(\mathbf B+\mathbf C)=\mathbf A\times\mathbf B+\mathbf A\times\mathbf C.

Parallel vectors, including antiparallel ones, have zero cross product. The cross product of a vector with itself is the zero vector 0\mathbf 0. When the cross product vanishes, its direction is undefined; the area interpretation still works.

1.1.4 Components and a worked angle calculation

Components are projections onto the basis vectors:

A=Axx^+Ayy^+Azz^,Ax=A·x^.\mathbf A=A_x\hat{\mathbf x}+A_y\hat{\mathbf y}+A_z\hat{\mathbf z},\qquad A_x=\mathbf A\cdot\hat{\mathbf x}.

The other components are obtained in the same way. Addition and scalar multiplication act component by component. Orthonormality gives

A·B=AxBx+AyBy+AzBz,A=Ax2+Ay2+Az2.\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z,\qquad A=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2+A_z^2}.

In the right-handed basis, x^×y^=z^\hat{\mathbf x}\times\hat{\mathbf y}=\hat{\mathbf z} and its cyclic versions hold; swapping the factors changes the sign. Thus

A×B=(AyBz-AzBy)x^+(AzBx-AxBz)y^+(AxBy-AyBx)z^.\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=(A_yB_z-A_zB_y)\hat{\mathbf x}+(A_zB_x-A_xB_z)\hat{\mathbf y}+(A_xB_y-A_yB_x)\hat{\mathbf z}.

The determinant is a compact mnemonic for this expansion:

A×B=|x^y^z^AxAyAzBxByBz|.\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{\mathbf x}&\hat{\mathbf y}&\hat{\mathbf z}\\A_x&A_y&A_z\\B_x&B_y&B_z\end{vmatrix}.

Worked example. For A=(1,0,1)\mathbf A=(1,0,1) and B=(0,1,1)\mathbf B=(0,1,1), the dot product is 11 and both magnitudes are 2\sqrt{2}. Therefore cosθ=1/2\cos\theta=1/2, giving θ=60°\theta=60^{\circ}.

1.1.5 Triple products: volume and the importance of brackets

The scalar triple product is unchanged by cyclic permutations:

A·(B×C)=B·(C×A)=C·(A×B).\mathbf A\cdot(\mathbf B\times\mathbf C)=\mathbf B\cdot(\mathbf C\times\mathbf A)=\mathbf C\cdot(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B).

Its absolute value is the parallelepiped volume; its sign records orientation. Swapping any two vectors reverses the sign. In components,

A·(B×C)=|AxAyAzBxByBzCxCyCz|.\mathbf A\cdot(\mathbf B\times\mathbf C)=\begin{vmatrix}A_x&A_y&A_z\\B_x&B_y&B_z\\C_x&C_y&C_z\end{vmatrix}.

For vector triple products, the BAC–CAB identity gives

A×(B×C)=B(A·C)-C(A·B).\mathbf A\times(\mathbf B\times\mathbf C)=\mathbf B(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf C)-\mathbf C(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B).

Changing the brackets changes the expression:

(A×B)×C=B(A·C)-A(B·C).(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)\times\mathbf C=\mathbf B(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf C)-\mathbf A(\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf C).

Cross products are therefore not associative. The first result lies in the plane spanned by B,C\mathbf B,\mathbf C; the second lies in the plane spanned by A,B\mathbf A,\mathbf B.

Two further identities can be reduced using the same rule:

A×[B×(C×D)]=B[A·(C×D)]-(A·B)(C×D).\mathbf A\times[\mathbf B\times(\mathbf C\times\mathbf D)]=\mathbf B[\mathbf A\cdot(\mathbf C\times\mathbf D)]-(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B)(\mathbf C\times\mathbf D).
(A×B)·(C×D)=(A·C)(B·D)-(A·D)(B·C).(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)\cdot(\mathbf C\times\mathbf D)=(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf C)(\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf D)-(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf D)(\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf C).

1.1.6 Source point and field point

In a field calculation, r′\mathbf r^{\prime} locates the source and r\mathbf r locates the point where the field is evaluated. Define the separation vector from source to field point as

R=r-r′,R=|r-r′|,R^=r-r′R.\mathbf R=\mathbf r-\mathbf r^{\prime},\qquad R=|\mathbf r-\mathbf r^{\prime}|,\qquad \hat{\mathbf R}=\frac{\mathbf r-\mathbf r^{\prime}}{R}.

This direction matters: exchanging the two points reverses R\mathbf R. The unit vector requires R≠0R\ne0. In later source integrals, derivatives with respect to r\mathbf r act while the source coordinate is held fixed.

1.2 Differential calculus

1.2.1 Gradient: from a total differential to steepest increase

For a differentiable scalar field T(x,y,z)T(x,y,z), a small displacement produces the first-order change described by

dT=∂T∂xdx+∂T∂ydy+∂T∂zdz=∇T·dr.dT=\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}dx+\frac{\partial T}{\partial y}dy+\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}dz=\nabla T\cdot d\mathbf r.

Here dr=dxx^+dyy^+dzz^d\mathbf r=dx\hat{\mathbf x}+dy\hat{\mathbf y}+dz\hat{\mathbf z} and

∇T=∂T∂xx^+∂T∂yy^+∂T∂zz^.\nabla T=\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}\hat{\mathbf x}+\frac{\partial T}{\partial y}\hat{\mathbf y}+\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\hat{\mathbf z}.

For a finite displacement, the corresponding relation is a local linear approximation, not an exact formula for an arbitrary distance. Along a unit direction u^\hat{\mathbf u}, with path distance ss,

dTds=∇T·u^=|∇T|cosθ.\frac{dT}{ds}=\nabla T\cdot\hat{\mathbf u}=|\nabla T|\cos\theta.

If the gradient is nonzero, its direction gives the steepest increase per unit distance and its magnitude gives that maximum rate. A direction perpendicular to the gradient has zero first-order change. For a temperature field measured in kelvin and positions in metres, the gradient has units of kelvin per metre.

Stationary does not necessarily mean extreme. At a differentiable interior point, ∇T=0\nabla T=\mathbf 0 means all first-order directional changes vanish. It does not by itself establish a maximum or minimum: there can be a saddle or a stationary inflection. For example, T=x2-y2T=x^2-y^2 has zero gradient at the origin but increases along one axis and decreases along the other.

1.2.2 Del is an operator, not an ordinary vector

In Cartesian coordinates,

∇=x^∂∂x+y^∂∂y+z^∂∂z.\nabla=\hat{\mathbf x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\hat{\mathbf y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\hat{\mathbf z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

The resemblance to a vector is useful, but its components differentiate the functions on which they act. Moving factors or applying an algebraic identity without tracking those derivatives can give a wrong result.

OperationInputOutputLocal interpretation
∇T\nabla TScalar fieldVector fieldDirection and rate of steepest increase
∇·v\nabla\cdot\mathbf vVector fieldScalar fieldNet outward flux per unit volume
∇×v\nabla\times\mathbf vVector fieldVector fieldOriented circulation per unit area

1.2.3 Divergence: local outflow, not just arrow size

∇·v=∂vx∂x+∂vy∂y+∂vz∂z.\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z}.

Divergence measures the limiting net outward flux through a small closed surface divided by its enclosed volume. Positive values indicate local source-like behavior and negative values local sink-like behavior. It is evaluated at each point; a positive value does not require a singular source located there.

For example, v=κ(x,y,z)\mathbf v=\kappa(x,y,z) with constant κ\kappa has divergence 3κ3\kappa everywhere. A large uniform vector field has zero divergence: its arrows are large, but their spatial derivatives vanish.

1.2.4 Curl: local circulation and its axis

∇×v=|x^y^z^∂/∂x∂/∂y∂/∂zvxvyvz|.\nabla\times\mathbf v=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{\mathbf x}&\hat{\mathbf y}&\hat{\mathbf z}\\\partial/\partial x&\partial/\partial y&\partial/\partial z\\v_x&v_y&v_z\end{vmatrix}.

Expanding gives

∇×v=(∂vz∂y-∂vy∂z)x^+(∂vx∂z-∂vz∂x)y^+(∂vy∂x-∂vx∂y)z^.\nabla\times\mathbf v=\left(\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial z}\right)\hat{\mathbf x}+\left(\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial x}\right)\hat{\mathbf y}+\left(\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\right)\hat{\mathbf z}.

Its component along a unit normal describes the limiting circulation per unit area around a small loop normal to that direction. The orientation follows the right-hand rule. “Local rotation” is a useful picture, but a curl value alone does not determine the shape of an entire field line.

As a check, the rigid-rotation velocity field v=Ω(-y,x,0)\mathbf v=\Omega(-y,x,0), with constant Ω\Omega, has ∇·v=0\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0 and ∇×v=2Ωz^\nabla\times\mathbf v=2\Omega\hat{\mathbf z}. Divergence and curl describe different aspects of the same field.

1.2.5 Product rules: let each derivative act on each factor

Gradient, divergence, and curl are linear and commute with multiplication by a constant scalar. For products of varying fields, extra terms appear. The six useful product rules are

∇(fg)=f∇g+g∇f.\nabla(fg)=f\nabla g+g\nabla f.
∇(A·B)=(A·∇)B+(B·∇)A+A×(∇×B)+B×(∇×A).\nabla(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B)=(\mathbf A\cdot\nabla)\mathbf B+(\mathbf B\cdot\nabla)\mathbf A+\mathbf A\times(\nabla\times\mathbf B)+\mathbf B\times(\nabla\times\mathbf A).
∇·(fA)=f(∇·A)+A·∇f.\nabla\cdot(f\mathbf A)=f(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)+\mathbf A\cdot\nabla f.
∇·(A×B)=B·(∇×A)-A·(∇×B).\nabla\cdot(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)=\mathbf B\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf A)-\mathbf A\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf B).
∇×(fA)=f(∇×A)+(∇f)×A.\nabla\times(f\mathbf A)=f(\nabla\times\mathbf A)+(\nabla f)\times\mathbf A.
∇×(A×B)=(B·∇)A-(A·∇)B+A(∇·B)-B(∇·A).\nabla\times(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)=(\mathbf B\cdot\nabla)\mathbf A-(\mathbf A\cdot\nabla)\mathbf B+\mathbf A(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B)-\mathbf B(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A).

The directional operator in these formulas means

(A·∇)B=Ax∂B∂x+Ay∂B∂y+Az∂B∂z.(\mathbf A\cdot\nabla)\mathbf B=A_x\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial x}+A_y\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial y}+A_z\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial z}.

It returns a vector; the derivatives act on B\mathbf B here. A reliable derivation strategy is to expand one Cartesian component, apply the ordinary one-variable product rule, and regroup. For instance, expanding ∂(fAx)/∂x\partial(fA_x)/\partial x and adding the other two components gives the divergence product rule. MIT’s divergence product-rule discussion develops this approach.

1.2.6 Quotient rules and a sign check

Treat division by gg as multiplication by g-1g^{-1} and use ∇(g-1)=-∇g/g2\nabla(g^{-1})=-\nabla g/g^2. Where g≠0g\ne0,

∇(fg)=g∇f-f∇gg2.\nabla\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)=\frac{g\nabla f-f\nabla g}{g^2}.
∇·(Ag)=g(∇·A)-A·∇gg2.\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\mathbf A}{g}\right)=\frac{g(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)-\mathbf A\cdot\nabla g}{g^2}.
∇×(Ag)=g(∇×A)-(∇g)×Ag2=g(∇×A)+A×∇gg2.\nabla\times\left(\frac{\mathbf A}{g}\right)=\frac{g(\nabla\times\mathbf A)-(\nabla g)\times\mathbf A}{g^2}=\frac{g(\nabla\times\mathbf A)+\mathbf A\times\nabla g}{g^2}.

The order of the cross product controls the sign. The correction term is negative when written as (∇g)×A(\nabla g)\times\mathbf A and positive when written as A×∇g\mathbf A\times\nabla g. This follows directly from the curl product rule.

For a quick check, take A=x^\mathbf A=\hat{\mathbf x} and g=yg=y in the region y≠0y\ne0. Direct differentiation gives ∇×(x^/y)=z^/y2\nabla\times(\hat{\mathbf x}/y)=\hat{\mathbf z}/y^2, agreeing with the plus sign in the last form.

1.2.7 Second derivatives and the Laplacian

Taking the divergence of a scalar field’s gradient defines its scalar Laplacian:

∇2T=∇·(∇T)=∂2T∂x2+∂2T∂y2+∂2T∂z2.\nabla^2T=\nabla\cdot(\nabla T)=\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial x^2}+\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial y^2}+\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial z^2}.

For fields with continuous second partial derivatives, mixed derivatives commute. Their cancellation gives two important identities:

∇×(∇T)=0,∇·(∇×v)=0.\nabla\times(\nabla T)=\mathbf 0,\qquad \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf v)=0.

For example, the zz component of the first identity is ∂2T/∂x∂y-∂2T/∂y∂x=0\partial^2T/\partial x\partial y-\partial^2T/\partial y\partial x=0. The zero in the curl identity is a vector; the zero in the divergence identity is a scalar. Singular points must be examined separately.

In a fixed Cartesian basis, the vector Laplacian acts on each component:

∇2v=(∇2vx)x^+(∇2vy)y^+(∇2vz)z^.\nabla^2\mathbf v=(\nabla^2v_x)\hat{\mathbf x}+(\nabla^2v_y)\hat{\mathbf y}+(\nabla^2v_z)\hat{\mathbf z}.

The curl-of-curl identity relates the remaining second-order operations:

∇×(∇×v)=∇(∇·v)-∇2v.\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf v)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v)-\nabla^2\mathbf v.

In general, ∇2v\nabla^2\mathbf v is not the same as ∇(∇·v)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v). Their difference is a curl-of-curl term. For example, v=(y2,0,0)\mathbf v=(y^2,0,0) has ∇(∇·v)=0\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v)=\mathbf 0 but ∇2v=2x^\nabla^2\mathbf v=2\hat{\mathbf x}.

Coordinate caution. These compact component formulas use a fixed Cartesian basis. The scalar Laplacian also exists in cylindrical and spherical coordinates, but its component expression changes. The local unit vectors in those coordinates depend on position; applying the vector Laplacian requires the associated basis derivatives and coordinate factors. The geometric identities still hold in Euclidean space when the operators are expressed correctly.

Review checkpoints

  1. Use projections to explain why an antiparallel dot product is negative, while the cross product vanishes.
  2. Expand the cross product in components and check the sign of its middle component.
  3. Explain why a stationary point need not be an extremum.
  4. Compare divergence and curl for a uniform field, a radial expansion, and rigid rotation.
  5. Derive the curl quotient rule from the product rule, keeping the cross-product order explicit.
  6. Use the curl-of-curl identity to distinguish the vector Laplacian from the gradient of divergence.

My understanding. Vector products first encode geometry: projection, area, and oriented volume. Differential operators then describe local field behavior, with different input and output types. The safest way to use their identities is to keep track of what each derivative acts on, the order of cross products, and the coordinate basis.

符号与范围。 粗体表示三维实矢量，A=|A|A=|\mathbf A| 表示大小。单位矢量 x^,y^,z^\hat{\mathbf x},\hat{\mathbf y},\hat{\mathbf z} 构成固定、右手、正交归一的笛卡尔基底。f,g,Tf,g,T 表示标量场，A,B,v\mathbf A,\mathbf B,\mathbf v 表示矢量场。微分公式用于场足够光滑的区域；商法则还要求 g≠0g\ne0。

1.1 矢量代数

1.1.1 矢量、标量与基本运算

矢量具有大小和方向。自由矢量可以平移而不改变矢量本身，但这并不意味着位置在物理中不重要：矢量场会给每一个位置分配一个矢量。标量没有空间方向，但其数值可以为正、负或零，也可以带有单位。

矢量加法采用首尾相接的作图方式；减法相当于加上反向矢量。乘以负标量会反转方向：

A+B=B+A,(A+B)+C=A+(B+C).\mathbf A+\mathbf B=\mathbf B+\mathbf A,\qquad (\mathbf A+\mathbf B)+\mathbf C=\mathbf A+(\mathbf B+\mathbf C).
A-B=A+(-B),a(A+B)=aA+aB.\mathbf A-\mathbf B=\mathbf A+(-\mathbf B),\qquad a(\mathbf A+\mathbf B)=a\mathbf A+a\mathbf B.

1.1.2 点积：投影与夹角

对于夹角为 θ\theta 的非零矢量，

A·B=ABcosθ.\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=AB\cos\theta.

结果是标量：一个矢量的大小，乘以另一个矢量沿其方向的带符号投影。夹角为锐角时结果为正，垂直时为零，钝角时为负。因此，平行但方向相反的矢量点积为 -AB-AB，而不是 ABAB。

A·B=B·A,A·(B+C)=A·B+A·C.\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf A,\qquad \mathbf A\cdot(\mathbf B+\mathbf C)=\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B+\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf C.

利用 A·A=A2\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf A=A^2，令 C=A-B\mathbf C=\mathbf A-\mathbf B，可以得到一个几何检查：

C2=(A-B)·(A-B)=A2+B2-2ABcosθ.C^2=(\mathbf A-\mathbf B)\cdot(\mathbf A-\mathbf B)=A^2+B^2-2AB\cos\theta.

1.1.3 叉积：带方向的面积

对于不平行的矢量，

A×B=ABsinθn^.\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=AB\sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n}.

单位法向量 n^\hat{\mathbf n} 由右手定则确定：手指从第一个矢量弯向第二个矢量。叉积的大小等于两矢量张成的平行四边形面积。交换顺序会反转法向：

B×A=-A×B,A×(B+C)=A×B+A×C.\mathbf B\times\mathbf A=-\mathbf A\times\mathbf B,\qquad \mathbf A\times(\mathbf B+\mathbf C)=\mathbf A\times\mathbf B+\mathbf A\times\mathbf C.

平行矢量，包括方向相反的平行矢量，叉积均为零。一个矢量与自身的叉积为零矢量 0\mathbf 0。当叉积为零时，它的方向没有定义，但面积的解释仍然成立。

1.1.4 分量表示与夹角计算例子

分量就是矢量在基底方向上的投影：

A=Axx^+Ayy^+Azz^,Ax=A·x^.\mathbf A=A_x\hat{\mathbf x}+A_y\hat{\mathbf y}+A_z\hat{\mathbf z},\qquad A_x=\mathbf A\cdot\hat{\mathbf x}.

其余分量同理。加法和数乘逐分量进行，正交归一性给出

A·B=AxBx+AyBy+AzBz,A=Ax2+Ay2+Az2.\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B=A_xB_x+A_yB_y+A_zB_z,\qquad A=\sqrt{A_x^2+A_y^2+A_z^2}.

在右手基底中，x^×y^=z^\hat{\mathbf x}\times\hat{\mathbf y}=\hat{\mathbf z}，循环置换后的关系也成立；交换顺序改变符号。因此

A×B=(AyBz-AzBy)x^+(AzBx-AxBz)y^+(AxBy-AyBx)z^.\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=(A_yB_z-A_zB_y)\hat{\mathbf x}+(A_zB_x-A_xB_z)\hat{\mathbf y}+(A_xB_y-A_yB_x)\hat{\mathbf z}.

行列式是记忆这一展开式的简洁写法：

A×B=|x^y^z^AxAyAzBxByBz|.\mathbf A\times\mathbf B=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{\mathbf x}&\hat{\mathbf y}&\hat{\mathbf z}\\A_x&A_y&A_z\\B_x&B_y&B_z\end{vmatrix}.

计算例子。 取 A=(1,0,1)\mathbf A=(1,0,1)、B=(0,1,1)\mathbf B=(0,1,1)，点积为 11，两个矢量的大小均为 2\sqrt{2}。于是 cosθ=1/2\cos\theta=1/2，得到 θ=60°\theta=60^{\circ}。

1.1.5 三重积：体积与括号的重要性

标量三重积在循环置换下不变：

A·(B×C)=B·(C×A)=C·(A×B).\mathbf A\cdot(\mathbf B\times\mathbf C)=\mathbf B\cdot(\mathbf C\times\mathbf A)=\mathbf C\cdot(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B).

其绝对值等于平行六面体的体积，正负号记录取向。交换任意两个矢量，符号反转。分量形式为

A·(B×C)=|AxAyAzBxByBzCxCyCz|.\mathbf A\cdot(\mathbf B\times\mathbf C)=\begin{vmatrix}A_x&A_y&A_z\\B_x&B_y&B_z\\C_x&C_y&C_z\end{vmatrix}.

矢量三重积由 BAC–CAB 恒等式给出：

A×(B×C)=B(A·C)-C(A·B).\mathbf A\times(\mathbf B\times\mathbf C)=\mathbf B(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf C)-\mathbf C(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B).

改变括号位置，表达式也会变化：

(A×B)×C=B(A·C)-A(B·C).(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)\times\mathbf C=\mathbf B(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf C)-\mathbf A(\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf C).

因此叉积不满足结合律。第一个结果位于 B,C\mathbf B,\mathbf C 张成的平面，第二个位于 A,B\mathbf A,\mathbf B 张成的平面。

以下两条恒等式也可以用同一规则化简：

A×[B×(C×D)]=B[A·(C×D)]-(A·B)(C×D).\mathbf A\times[\mathbf B\times(\mathbf C\times\mathbf D)]=\mathbf B[\mathbf A\cdot(\mathbf C\times\mathbf D)]-(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B)(\mathbf C\times\mathbf D).
(A×B)·(C×D)=(A·C)(B·D)-(A·D)(B·C).(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)\cdot(\mathbf C\times\mathbf D)=(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf C)(\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf D)-(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf D)(\mathbf B\cdot\mathbf C).

1.1.6 源点与场点

在场的计算中，r′\mathbf r^{\prime} 表示源的位置，r\mathbf r 表示要计算场的位置。定义从源点指向场点的分离矢量：

R=r-r′,R=|r-r′|,R^=r-r′R.\mathbf R=\mathbf r-\mathbf r^{\prime},\qquad R=|\mathbf r-\mathbf r^{\prime}|,\qquad \hat{\mathbf R}=\frac{\mathbf r-\mathbf r^{\prime}}{R}.

这个方向约定很重要：交换两个点，R\mathbf R 就会反向。单位矢量要求 R≠0R\ne0。后续对源作积分时，若对场点坐标 r\mathbf r 求导，就应保持源坐标不变。

1.2 微分运算

1.2.1 梯度：从全微分到增长最快的方向

对于可微的标量场 T(x,y,z)T(x,y,z)，小位移引起的一阶变化由下式描述：

dT=∂T∂xdx+∂T∂ydy+∂T∂zdz=∇T·dr.dT=\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}dx+\frac{\partial T}{\partial y}dy+\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}dz=\nabla T\cdot d\mathbf r.

其中 dr=dxx^+dyy^+dzz^d\mathbf r=dx\hat{\mathbf x}+dy\hat{\mathbf y}+dz\hat{\mathbf z}，而

∇T=∂T∂xx^+∂T∂yy^+∂T∂zz^.\nabla T=\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}\hat{\mathbf x}+\frac{\partial T}{\partial y}\hat{\mathbf y}+\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\hat{\mathbf z}.

对于有限位移，相应关系是局部线性近似，不能当作任意距离上的精确变化公式。沿单位方向 u^\hat{\mathbf u} 运动，以 ss 表示路径距离，有

dTds=∇T·u^=|∇T|cosθ.\frac{dT}{ds}=\nabla T\cdot\hat{\mathbf u}=|\nabla T|\cos\theta.

当梯度非零时，其方向是单位距离上增长最快的方向，其大小给出这个最大增长率。与梯度垂直的方向，一阶变化为零。若温度用开尔文、位置用米表示，则温度梯度的单位为开尔文每米。

驻点不一定是极值点。 在可微的内部点，∇T=0\nabla T=\mathbf 0 表示所有一阶方向变化都为零，但不能单凭它确定极大值或极小值：也可能出现鞍点或驻拐点。例如 T=x2-y2T=x^2-y^2 在原点的梯度为零，却沿一个坐标轴增加、沿另一个坐标轴减少。

1.2.2 Del 是算符，不是普通矢量

在笛卡尔坐标下，

∇=x^∂∂x+y^∂∂y+z^∂∂z.\nabla=\hat{\mathbf x}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\hat{\mathbf y}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\hat{\mathbf z}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

它看起来像矢量，这一形式很有用；但它的分量会对所作用的函数求导。移动因子或套用代数恒等式时，若不追踪这些导数，便可能得到错误结果。

运算输入输出局部含义
∇T\nabla T标量场矢量场增长最快的方向与变化率
∇·v\nabla\cdot\mathbf v矢量场标量场单位体积的净向外通量
∇×v\nabla\times\mathbf v矢量场矢量场单位面积的带方向环流

1.2.3 散度：局部流出，而非箭头大小

∇·v=∂vx∂x+∂vy∂y+∂vz∂z.\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial z}.

散度表示：一个小闭合面的净向外通量除以所围体积，在体积趋于零时的极限。正值表示局部类似源的行为，负值表示局部类似汇的行为。它在每一点都有定义；散度为正并不要求那个位置存在一个奇异的点源。

例如，v=κ(x,y,z)\mathbf v=\kappa(x,y,z) 中 κ\kappa 为常量，则散度在各处均为 3κ3\kappa。一个很大的均匀矢量场却具有零散度：箭头虽大，但空间导数为零。

1.2.4 旋度：局部环流及其轴向

∇×v=|x^y^z^∂/∂x∂/∂y∂/∂zvxvyvz|.\nabla\times\mathbf v=\begin{vmatrix}\hat{\mathbf x}&\hat{\mathbf y}&\hat{\mathbf z}\\\partial/\partial x&\partial/\partial y&\partial/\partial z\\v_x&v_y&v_z\end{vmatrix}.

展开后得到

∇×v=(∂vz∂y-∂vy∂z)x^+(∂vx∂z-∂vz∂x)y^+(∂vy∂x-∂vx∂y)z^.\nabla\times\mathbf v=\left(\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial z}\right)\hat{\mathbf x}+\left(\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial z}-\frac{\partial v_z}{\partial x}\right)\hat{\mathbf y}+\left(\frac{\partial v_y}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial y}\right)\hat{\mathbf z}.

旋度沿某单位法向的分量，描述与该法向垂直的小环路上，单位面积环流的极限。方向由右手定则决定。“局部旋转”是有用的物理图像，但仅靠某点的旋度，不能确定整条场线的形状。

作为检查，刚体转动速度场 v=Ω(-y,x,0)\mathbf v=\Omega(-y,x,0) 中 Ω\Omega 为常量，则 ∇·v=0\nabla\cdot\mathbf v=0、∇×v=2Ωz^\nabla\times\mathbf v=2\Omega\hat{\mathbf z}。散度和旋度描述同一个场的不同方面。

1.2.5 乘积法则：让导数作用到每个因子

梯度、散度和旋度均为线性运算，可以提出常量标量因子。但对于随位置变化的因子，会出现额外项。六条常用乘积法则为

∇(fg)=f∇g+g∇f.\nabla(fg)=f\nabla g+g\nabla f.
∇(A·B)=(A·∇)B+(B·∇)A+A×(∇×B)+B×(∇×A).\nabla(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B)=(\mathbf A\cdot\nabla)\mathbf B+(\mathbf B\cdot\nabla)\mathbf A+\mathbf A\times(\nabla\times\mathbf B)+\mathbf B\times(\nabla\times\mathbf A).
∇·(fA)=f(∇·A)+A·∇f.\nabla\cdot(f\mathbf A)=f(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)+\mathbf A\cdot\nabla f.
∇·(A×B)=B·(∇×A)-A·(∇×B).\nabla\cdot(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)=\mathbf B\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf A)-\mathbf A\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf B).
∇×(fA)=f(∇×A)+(∇f)×A.\nabla\times(f\mathbf A)=f(\nabla\times\mathbf A)+(\nabla f)\times\mathbf A.
∇×(A×B)=(B·∇)A-(A·∇)B+A(∇·B)-B(∇·A).\nabla\times(\mathbf A\times\mathbf B)=(\mathbf B\cdot\nabla)\mathbf A-(\mathbf A\cdot\nabla)\mathbf B+\mathbf A(\nabla\cdot\mathbf B)-\mathbf B(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A).

这些公式中的方向微分算符表示

(A·∇)B=Ax∂B∂x+Ay∂B∂y+Az∂B∂z.(\mathbf A\cdot\nabla)\mathbf B=A_x\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial x}+A_y\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial y}+A_z\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial z}.

其结果是矢量，右侧的导数在这里作用于 B\mathbf B。可靠的推导方法，是展开一个笛卡尔分量，应用普通的一元乘积法则，再将结果重组。例如，展开 ∂(fAx)/∂x\partial(fA_x)/\partial x，再加上另外两个分量，便得到散度乘积法则。MIT 的散度乘积法则说明 展示了这一方法。

1.2.6 商法则与符号检查

将除以 gg 看成乘以 g-1g^{-1}，并使用 ∇(g-1)=-∇g/g2\nabla(g^{-1})=-\nabla g/g^2。在 g≠0g\ne0 的区域，有

∇(fg)=g∇f-f∇gg2.\nabla\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)=\frac{g\nabla f-f\nabla g}{g^2}.
∇·(Ag)=g(∇·A)-A·∇gg2.\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\mathbf A}{g}\right)=\frac{g(\nabla\cdot\mathbf A)-\mathbf A\cdot\nabla g}{g^2}.
∇×(Ag)=g(∇×A)-(∇g)×Ag2=g(∇×A)+A×∇gg2.\nabla\times\left(\frac{\mathbf A}{g}\right)=\frac{g(\nabla\times\mathbf A)-(\nabla g)\times\mathbf A}{g^2}=\frac{g(\nabla\times\mathbf A)+\mathbf A\times\nabla g}{g^2}.

叉积顺序决定符号。 修正项若写成 (∇g)×A(\nabla g)\times\mathbf A，前面取负号；若写成 A×∇g\mathbf A\times\nabla g，前面取正号。这直接来自旋度乘积法则。

快速检查：在 y≠0y\ne0 的区域取 A=x^\mathbf A=\hat{\mathbf x}、g=yg=y，直接求导得到 ∇×(x^/y)=z^/y2\nabla\times(\hat{\mathbf x}/y)=\hat{\mathbf z}/y^2，与最后一种写法中的正号一致。

1.2.7 二阶导数与拉普拉斯算符

对标量场的梯度取散度，定义其标量拉普拉斯算符：

∇2T=∇·(∇T)=∂2T∂x2+∂2T∂y2+∂2T∂z2.\nabla^2T=\nabla\cdot(\nabla T)=\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial x^2}+\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial y^2}+\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial z^2}.

若场的二阶偏导连续，则混合偏导可以交换次序。相应项相消，得到两个重要恒等式：

∇×(∇T)=0,∇·(∇×v)=0.\nabla\times(\nabla T)=\mathbf 0,\qquad \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf v)=0.

例如，第一个恒等式的 zz 分量为 ∂2T/∂x∂y-∂2T/∂y∂x=0\partial^2T/\partial x\partial y-\partial^2T/\partial y\partial x=0。旋度式中的零是矢量，散度式中的零是标量。奇异点需要单独分析。

在固定笛卡尔基底中，矢量拉普拉斯逐分量作用：

∇2v=(∇2vx)x^+(∇2vy)y^+(∇2vz)z^.\nabla^2\mathbf v=(\nabla^2v_x)\hat{\mathbf x}+(\nabla^2v_y)\hat{\mathbf y}+(\nabla^2v_z)\hat{\mathbf z}.

旋度的旋度将其余二阶运算联系起来：

∇×(∇×v)=∇(∇·v)-∇2v.\nabla\times(\nabla\times\mathbf v)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v)-\nabla^2\mathbf v.

一般来说，∇2v\nabla^2\mathbf v 不等于 ∇(∇·v)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v)，两者之差涉及旋度的旋度。例如，v=(y2,0,0)\mathbf v=(y^2,0,0) 有 ∇(∇·v)=0\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf v)=\mathbf 0，但 ∇2v=2x^\nabla^2\mathbf v=2\hat{\mathbf x}。

坐标提醒。 上述简洁分量式采用固定笛卡尔基底。标量拉普拉斯在柱坐标和球坐标中同样存在，但表达式会变化。那些坐标中的局部单位矢量依赖位置，计算矢量拉普拉斯时必须计入相应的基矢导数和坐标因子。在欧几里得空间中，只要正确表示各算符，几何恒等式仍然成立。

复习检查

  1. 用投影解释：为什么反向平行矢量的点积为负，而叉积为零？
  2. 用分量展开叉积，检查中间分量的符号。
  3. 解释为什么驻点不一定是极值点。
  4. 对比均匀场、径向膨胀和刚体转动的散度与旋度。
  5. 从乘积法则推导旋度商法则，明确保留叉积顺序。
  6. 用旋度的旋度恒等式，区分矢量拉普拉斯与散度的梯度。

我的理解。 矢量乘积首先记录几何：投影、面积与带方向的体积。微分算符则进一步描述场的局部行为，并具有不同的输入、输出类型。使用恒等式时，最稳妥的方法是始终追踪导数作用于什么、叉积的顺序是什么，以及采用什么坐标基底。
