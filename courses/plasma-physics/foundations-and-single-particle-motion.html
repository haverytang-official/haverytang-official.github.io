Study guide · Sections 01–03

Plasma fundamentals and single-particle motion

These three sections connect collective plasma behavior, charged-particle motion, and the basic ideas behind magnetic confinement. Follow three questions:

  1. What makes an ionized gas behave as a plasma?
  2. How does a charged particle move in electromagnetic fields?
  3. How do those motions help us understand magnetic confinement?

Notation. SI units are used throughout. Temperature TT is in kelvin, so thermal energy is kBTk_B T; a temperature quoted in eV refers to the value of kBTk_B T in that energy unit. The elementary charge ee is positive; the particle charge qq includes its sign. A subscript ss labels a species. Bold symbols are vectors, BB is the magnetic-field magnitude, and b=B/B\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{B}/B. The safety factor is written qsafq_{\mathrm{saf}} to distinguish it from electric charge.

01 · Plasma overview

1.1 An ionized gas with collective behavior

A plasma contains mobile charged particles, usually electrons and ions, and may also contain neutral atoms or molecules. Ionization supplies the charges; collective behavior describes how their motion couples through electromagnetic fields. A disturbance can therefore influence many particles, rather than only the two participants in a local collision.

The feedback is essential: particle motion produces charge and current densities, these generate fields, and those fields change particle motion. This is why a plasma can support shielding, oscillations, and waves.

Thermal ionization becomes easier as temperature rises. In local thermodynamic equilibrium, the Saha relation has the schematic form

neninn∝T3/2exp(-EikBT).\frac{n_e n_i}{n_n}\propto T^{3/2}\exp\left(-\frac{E_i}{k_B T}\right).

Here nnn_n is the neutral density and EiE_i the ionization energy; statistical factors are suppressed. For a given temperature, increasing the total density generally lowers the equilibrium ionization fraction in the ideal Saha model. This is not a universal formula for a discharge: nonthermal electrons, external radiation, and nonequilibrium processes can determine its ionization balance.

1.2 Temperature, energy, and different species

For an isotropic Maxwellian species, the mean random kinetic energy per particle is 3kBT/23k_B T/2. Its thermal energy density is consequently 3nkBT/23n k_B T/2. A dilute plasma can have a high temperature without a large thermal energy density; the total energy also depends on its volume.

1eV=1.602176634×10-19J,1eVkB≃11605K.1\,\mathrm{eV}=1.602176634\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J},\qquad \frac{1\,\mathrm{eV}}{k_B}\simeq11605\,\mathrm{K}.

Electrons and ions need not have the same temperature. Energy exchange may be slower than heating or losses, leaving Te≠TiT_e\ne T_i. A magnetized distribution may also have different temperatures parallel and perpendicular to the field. Temperature is then a description of the velocity distribution, rather than a single number characterizing every particle.

1.3 Debye shielding: a derivation and its meaning

Place a weak positive test charge QQ in an otherwise uniform plasma. Electrons move toward it and form a surrounding negative charge perturbation. The combined charge distribution weakens the electric potential at large distances.

For a simple static model, hold singly charged ions fixed at density n0n_0, and assume Maxwellian electrons at temperature TeT_e with a Boltzmann response:

ne=n0exp(eφkBTe)≃n0(1+eφkBTe).n_e=n_0\exp\left(\frac{e\phi}{k_B T_e}\right)\simeq n_0\left(1+\frac{e\phi}{k_B T_e}\right).

The approximation requires |eφ|≪kBTe|e\phi|\ll k_B T_e. The induced plasma charge density is then ρind≃-n0e2φ/(kBTe)\rho_{\mathrm{ind}}\simeq-n_0e^2\phi/(k_B T_e). Substituting it and the test charge into Poisson’s equation gives

∇2φ-φλDe2=-Qδ(r)ε0,λDe=ε0kBTen0e2.\nabla^2\phi-\frac{\phi}{\lambda_{De}^2}=-\frac{Q\delta(\mathbf{r})}{\epsilon_0},\qquad \lambda_{De}=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_0 k_B T_e}{n_0e^2}}.

For spherical symmetry, with the potential vanishing at infinity, the solution is

φ(r)=Q4πε0rexp(-rλDe).\phi(r)=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}\exp\left(-\frac{r}{\lambda_{De}}\right).

The electron Debye length λDe\lambda_{De} is the characteristic shielding distance. Higher temperature makes it harder to localize the electrons; higher density supplies more charges for screening. Accordingly, λDe∝Te/n0\lambda_{De}\propto\sqrt{T_e/n_0}.

Shielding is gradual: the field does not suddenly vanish at one Debye length. The linear result also need not describe the immediate vicinity of a strong test charge. If several Maxwellian species respond, their contributions add as λD-2=∑snsqs2/(ε0kBTs)\lambda_D^{-2}=\sum_s n_s q_s^2/(\epsilon_0 k_B T_s). See Fitzpatrick’s Debye shielding discussion for the equilibrium screening model.

1.4 Quasineutrality is a scale-dependent approximation

On scales much larger than the shielding length, a bulk plasma is often approximately neutral:

ne≃∑iZini,L≫λD.n_e\simeq\sum_i Z_i n_i,\qquad L\gg\lambda_D.

Quasineutrality does not mean that the electric field is zero, or that every small volume contains exactly equal positive and negative charge. Small imbalances can sustain important fields. Sheaths and other Debye-scale structures need a treatment that allows charge separation. Nonneutral plasmas also exist; the large-scale quasineutral model is a useful regime, not a definition covering every plasma.

1.5 Plasma oscillations and response time

Imagine displacing an electron fluid a small distance xx relative to fixed ions in a cold, uniform slab. The resulting electric field provides a restoring force. The electron equation becomes

d2xdt2+ωpe2x=0,ωpe=nee2ε0me.\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}+\omega_{pe}^2x=0,\qquad \omega_{pe}=\sqrt{\frac{n_e e^2}{\epsilon_0m_e}}.

This is the electron plasma angular frequency. The characteristic response time is of order 1/ωpe1/\omega_{pe}, while the full oscillation period is 2π/ωpe2\pi/\omega_{pe}. These two quantities should not be confused. For ions with charge ZieZ_i e, the analogous species frequency is ωpi=niZi2e2/(ε0mi)\omega_{pi}=\sqrt{n_i Z_i^2e^2/(\epsilon_0m_i)}; ions generally respond more slowly because of their larger mass.

Finite temperature and spatial variation introduce wave physics beyond this simple oscillator. The model nevertheless shows how a small charge displacement can create a collective restoring field.

1.6 Many particles, many small deflections

For a weakly coupled plasma, many particles occupy a Debye sphere:

ND=4π3neλD3≫1.N_D=\frac{4\pi}{3}n_e\lambda_D^3\gg1.

The large number makes a smooth collective description useful. Coulomb collisions still occur, usually through the accumulation of many small-angle deflections. For a fixed species and charge state in the usual weak-coupling regime, a characteristic Coulomb collision frequency scales as ν∝nlnΛ/T3/2\nu\propto n\ln\Lambda/T^{3/2}, with lnΛ\ln\Lambda the Coulomb logarithm. Its precise definition and prefactor depend on the relaxation process.

Whether collisions matter depends on the process being studied. An oscillation can be approximately collisionless when its frequency greatly exceeds the relevant collision frequency. Neutral collisions can dominate some partially ionized plasmas; one should compare their timescale with the dynamics of interest rather than apply a single universal inequality.

The self-consistent electromagnetic framework is Maxwell’s equations:

∇·E=ρε0,∇·B=0.\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}=\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0},\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0.
∇×E=-∂B∂t,∇×B=μ0J+μ0ε0∂E∂t.\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t},\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf{B}=\mu_0\mathbf{J}+\mu_0\epsilon_0\frac{\partial\mathbf{E}}{\partial t}.

Review check. Explain the difference between temperature and energy density; derive the screened potential; distinguish 1/ωpe1/\omega_{pe} from 2π/ωpe2\pi/\omega_{pe}; and identify when quasineutrality or a collisionless approximation can be used.

02 · Single-particle motion I

2.1 Choosing a level of description

A test-particle model follows a trajectory in prescribed fields. A kinetic model follows a distribution fs(r,v,t)f_s(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{v},t) and includes how particles populate velocity space. A fluid model follows moments such as density, bulk velocity, and pressure; MHD is a particular fluid approximation with its own assumptions.

For a classical, nonrelativistic test particle,

drdt=v,mdvdt=q(E+v×B).\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}=\mathbf{v},\qquad m\frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt}=q(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}).

In a self-consistent kinetic calculation, ns=∫fsd3vn_s=\int f_s\,d^3v and J=∑sqs∫vfsd3v\mathbf{J}=\sum_s q_s\int\mathbf{v}f_s\,d^3v connect the particle distributions to the fields. Following one orbit in a specified field is a useful starting point, but it does not by itself determine a plasma equilibrium or transport coefficient.

2.2 Uniform magnetic field: gyromotion plus parallel motion

Take B=Bz^\mathbf{B}=B\hat{\mathbf{z}} and E=0\mathbf{E}=0. Define the signed frequency Ω=qB/m\Omega=qB/m and the positive cyclotron frequency ωc=|q|B/m\omega_c=|q|B/m. Then

dvxdt=Ωvy,dvydt=-Ωvx,dv∥dt=0.\frac{dv_x}{dt}=\Omega v_y,\qquad \frac{dv_y}{dt}=-\Omega v_x,\qquad \frac{dv_{\parallel}}{dt}=0.

The perpendicular velocity rotates at constant magnitude. The particle moves in a circle perpendicular to the field, with Larmor radius

ρL=v⊥ωc=mv⊥|q|B.\rho_L=\frac{v_{\perp}}{\omega_c}=\frac{m v_{\perp}}{|q|B}.

Parallel motion turns the circle into a helix. The sign of qq sets the rotation direction. Magnetic force changes direction but does no work, since v·(v×B)=0\mathbf{v}\cdot(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})=0. A uniform magnetic field therefore constrains perpendicular motion without preventing travel along the field.

2.3 The guiding center and its domain of validity

Separate the fast circular motion from its slowly moving center. To lowest order, the guiding center streams along the field and experiences perpendicular drifts. This approximation is useful when the orbit samples only a small change in the field during a gyroperiod:

ρLLB≪1,1ωcB|dBdt|≪1.\frac{\rho_L}{L_B}\ll1,\qquad \frac{1}{\omega_c B}\left|\frac{dB}{dt}\right|\ll1.

Here LBL_B is a characteristic field-variation length and the time derivative follows the relevant motion. Variation of the field direction and electric-field scales must also be slow enough. Near a magnetic null or a sharp gradient, a guiding-center description may fail.

The scalar magnetic moment is

μ=mv⊥22B=W⊥B.\mu=\frac{m v_{\perp}^2}{2B}=\frac{W_{\perp}}{B}.

It is approximately conserved under suitable slow variations. This conservation will connect particle motion to magnetic mirrors in Section 03.

2.4 Electric drift and a general force drift

For uniform static fields with a perpendicular electric component, the gyro-orbit translates at

vE=E×BB2.\mathbf{v}_E=\frac{\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}}{B^2}.

The result follows by setting the slow perpendicular force balance to E⊥+vE×B=0\mathbf{E}_{\perp}+\mathbf{v}_E\times\mathbf{B}=0. It is independent of particle charge and mass: electrons and ions share this drift in the same fields. A parallel electric field instead accelerates the particle along B\mathbf{B} through mdv∥/dt=qE∥m\,dv_{\parallel}/dt=qE_{\parallel}.

For another weak, slowly varying force F⊥\mathbf{F}_{\perp} acting on one particle,

vF=F×BqB2.\mathbf{v}_F=\frac{\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{B}}{qB^2}.

Only the perpendicular component contributes to this expression. Substituting F=qE\mathbf{F}=q\mathbf{E} recovers the electric drift and explains why the charge cancels. A force that does not reverse with charge generally gives opposite drift directions for positive and negative particles.

2.5 Gradient and curvature drifts

A field-strength gradient makes the two sides of a gyro-orbit slightly unequal. The associated effective force is -μ∇B-\mu\nabla B, giving

v∇B=μqB2B×∇B.\mathbf{v}_{\nabla B}=\frac{\mu}{qB^2}\mathbf{B}\times\nabla B.

Curvature is different: a particle streaming along a bent field line must turn even if the field magnitude is locally constant. With κ=(b·∇)b\boldsymbol{\kappa}=(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{b} pointing toward the local center of curvature,

vcurv=mv∥2qBb×κ.\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{curv}}=\frac{m v_{\parallel}^2}{qB}\mathbf{b}\times\boldsymbol{\kappa}.

Both are charge-dependent. The gradient drift depends on perpendicular energy, while the curvature drift depends on parallel energy. These are distinct contributions that can coexist. The leading expressions assume a magnetized particle and the guiding-center ordering. Fitzpatrick’s magnetic drift notes give these drift terms and their geometric interpretation.

In a simple toroidal field, Bφ∝1/RB_{\varphi}\propto1/R: the inner side has a stronger field. Gradient and curvature drifts then separate opposite charges vertically. The resulting electric field can drive a common drift across the torus. This gives a physical reason to add a poloidal field component and create helical field lines, rather than rely on a purely toroidal field for confinement.

2.6 Time-dependent fields: polarization and betatron effects

If E⊥\mathbf{E}_{\perp} varies slowly in time while B\mathbf{B} is uniform and stationary, the guiding center has an additional polarization drift:

vpol=mqB2dE⊥dt.\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pol}}=\frac{m}{qB^2}\frac{d\mathbf{E}_{\perp}}{dt}.

It represents the inertial response to a changing electric drift. Unlike vE\mathbf{v}_E, it depends on charge and mass. The derivative reduces to a time derivative for a spatially uniform electric field; a general inhomogeneous problem requires a consistent derivative along the guiding-center motion.

A changing magnetic field induces an electric field through Faraday’s law. If the change remains adiabatic and μ\mu is conserved,

W⊥=μB,dW⊥dt=μdBdt.W_{\perp}=\mu B,\qquad \frac{dW_{\perp}}{dt}=\mu\frac{dB}{dt}.

Increasing BB can therefore increase perpendicular kinetic energy: the betatron effect. The energy is supplied by the induced electric field. It does not imply that the magnetic part of the Lorentz force performs work.

2.7 Spatially varying and rapidly oscillating electric fields

An electric field that changes appreciably across a Larmor orbit is sampled at different positions during one rotation. The local electric-drift formula is then only the leading term; finite-Larmor-radius corrections become relevant when ρL/LE\rho_L/L_E is not small.

A separate averaging problem occurs in a rapidly oscillating electric field. For a nonrelativistic, effectively unmagnetized particle in E(r,t)=E0(r)cosωt\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t)=\mathbf{E}_0(\mathbf{r})\cos\omega t, with a slowly varying amplitude and a small oscillation displacement, the averaged force is

Fpond=-q24mω2∇|E0|2.\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{pond}}=-\frac{q^2}{4m\omega^2}\nabla|\mathbf{E}_0|^2.

Here E0\mathbf{E}_0 is the peak amplitude, rather than the RMS field. The squared charge makes the direction independent of the charge sign: the force points toward lower field intensity. A magnetized response or proximity to a resonance requires a different treatment; this expression should not be extended unchanged to those regimes. See Motion in Oscillating Fields.

03 · Single-particle motion II

3.1 Adiabatic invariants and nested timescales

An exact invariant follows from an exact property of the equations, such as a symmetry. An adiabatic invariant is approximately conserved when a periodic motion takes place in an environment that changes sufficiently slowly. For a one-dimensional periodic Hamiltonian system, its action is

J=∮pxdx.J=\oint p_x\,dx.

The integral encloses one orbit in phase space. Here pxp_x is canonical momentum; for an electromagnetic particle, p=mv+qA\mathbf{p}=m\mathbf{v}+q\mathbf{A}, rather than just mvm\mathbf{v}. Energy may change while an action remains approximately fixed. Fitzpatrick’s adiabatic-invariant discussion connects this action to the magnetic moment.

In a well-separated trapped-particle regime, gyromotion is fastest, bounce motion is slower, and drift or precession is slower still:

ωc≫ωb≫ωd.\omega_c\gg\omega_b\gg\omega_d.

The first invariant is μ=W⊥/B\mu=W_{\perp}/B. The second is a bounce action. A third can be associated with a closed, slow drift orbit and, in suitable configurations, related approximately to its enclosed magnetic flux. Conserving a slower action requires the environment to change slowly compared with that longer period; conservation of μ\mu alone does not guarantee conservation of every other action.

The flux interpretation is especially useful for magnetospheric drift shells. It is not a claim that every tokamak particle exactly preserves an arbitrarily chosen enclosed flux. Axisymmetry instead supplies conservation of canonical toroidal momentum, subject to the assumed field symmetry. See Third Adiabatic Invariant.

3.2 Magnetic mirrors: reflection without magnetic work

Consider a static, smoothly varying magnetic field with no electric field. At guiding-center order, total kinetic energy WW and magnetic moment μ\mu are conserved:

W=mv∥22+μB.W=\frac{m v_{\parallel}^2}{2}+\mu B.

As the particle enters a stronger field, W⊥=μBW_{\perp}=\mu B increases and the parallel kinetic energy decreases. This is a redistribution of a fixed total energy. Differentiating along the field line yields the mirror force

mdv∥dt=-μ∂B∂s,m\frac{dv_{\parallel}}{dt}=-\mu\frac{\partial B}{\partial s},

where ss measures distance along the field. A turning point occurs when v∥=0v_{\parallel}=0, or Bm=W/μB_m=W/\mu.

Let the pitch angle at a reference point of field strength B0B_0 be defined by sinα0=v⊥0/v\sin\alpha_0=v_{\perp0}/v, with α0\alpha_0 measured from B\mathbf{B}. Using μ=Wsin2α0/B0\mu=W\sin^2\alpha_0/B_0 gives

sin2α0=B0Bm.\sin^2\alpha_0=\frac{B_0}{B_m}.

If the maximum accessible field is BmaxB_{\max}, the boundary of the loss cone satisfies sin2αLC=B0/Bmax\sin^2\alpha_{\mathrm{LC}}=B_0/B_{\max}. Particles with smaller sin2α0\sin^2\alpha_0 are not reflected before reaching that maximum. A loss cone is a region of velocity space, around the parallel and antiparallel directions, not a cone-shaped opening in physical space.

Collisions can scatter trapped particles into the loss cone, so mirror confinement is not loss-free. Resonant perturbations and magnetic nulls can also undermine the assumptions supporting μ\mu conservation. The magnetic mirror discussion develops the trapping and loss-cone picture. The same basic interplay of gyration, reflection, and slow drift helps interpret particles trapped in Earth’s magnetic field.

3.3 The bounce action and slow compression

For a particle trapped between mirror points s1s_1 and s2s_2, in the simple zero-electric-field mirror model, the second invariant is

J∥=∮mv∥ds=2∫s1s22m[W-μB(s)]ds.J_{\parallel}=\oint m v_{\parallel}\,ds=2\int_{s_1}^{s_2}\sqrt{2m[W-\mu B(s)]}\,ds.

The closed integral follows the complete outward-and-return bounce in phase space; its two halves add, rather than cancel. It is approximately invariant when the configuration changes slowly compared with the bounce period and the orbit remains trapped, away from a separatrix crossing.

If the distance between reflecting boundaries decreases slowly, conserving the bounce action can increase parallel energy. In the idealized nearly uniform interior, J∥≃2m|v∥|LJ_{\parallel}\simeq2m|v_{\parallel}|L gives W∥∝L-2W_{\parallel}\propto L^{-2}. This is an illustration of adiabatic Fermi acceleration; a realistic mirror profile requires the full integral. See Second Adiabatic Invariant.

3.4 Tokamak geometry and trapped or passing particles

A tokamak combines toroidal and poloidal field components to form helical field lines. In the conventional picture, external coils supply the main toroidal field and the toroidal plasma current supplies much of the poloidal field. A stellarator generates its confining geometry primarily with external three-dimensional coils; this distinction alone does not rank their confinement or stability.

For a schematic circular flux surface, cylindrical coordinates (R,φ,Z)(R,\varphi,Z) and local coordinates (r,θ,φ)(r,\theta,\varphi) are related by

R=R0+rcosθ,Z=rsinθ.R=R_0+r\cos\theta,\qquad Z=r\sin\theta.

Here θ=0\theta=0 is the outboard midplane. In a large-aspect-ratio, toroidal-field-dominated approximation,

B(θ)≃Bref1+εtcosθ,εt=rR0≪1.B(\theta)\simeq\frac{B_{\mathrm{ref}}}{1+\epsilon_t\cos\theta},\qquad \epsilon_t=\frac{r}{R_0}\ll1.

The field is weaker on the outside and stronger on the inside. Ignoring collisions and significant electric-potential variations, the parallel velocity follows

v∥=±2m[W-μB(θ)].v_{\parallel}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}[W-\mu B(\theta)]}.

A passing particle has enough parallel energy to travel through the high-field region and complete a poloidal circuit. A trapped particle reflects before reaching it and bounces on the outboard side. Reflection is determined by its energy and pitch angle, not by a change in the sign of its charge.

3.5 Banana orbits are guiding-center orbits

Magnetic drifts displace a bouncing particle’s guiding center from an individual field line. Its projection into the poloidal plane can form a banana-shaped orbit. The banana is not the microscopic Larmor circle; rapid gyromotion remains superposed on the guiding-center trajectory.

For thermal particles in a simple circular, large-aspect-ratio tokamak, characteristic radial orbit widths have the scalings

Δpass∼qsafρs,Δbanana∼qsafρsεt,\Delta_{\mathrm{pass}}\sim q_{\mathrm{saf}}\rho_s,\qquad \Delta_{\mathrm{banana}}\sim\frac{q_{\mathrm{saf}}\rho_s}{\sqrt{\epsilon_t}},

with ρs=vth,s/ωc,s\rho_s=v_{\mathrm{th},s}/\omega_{c,s} and vth,s=2kBTs/msv_{\mathrm{th},s}=\sqrt{2k_B T_s/m_s}. These are order-of-magnitude estimates, not exact widths for every pitch angle. They become unreliable close to the trapped–passing boundary or when finite orbit width is no longer a small correction. The trapped and passing particle notes describe this idealized geometry and orbit classification.

Gyro-averaging removes the fast rotation to produce a guiding-center description. Bounce-averaging removes the back-and-forth motion to describe the slower evolution of a trapped orbit. The leading bounce-averaged parallel streaming can cancel without making all motion vanish: magnetic precession and electric drifts remain.

3.6 Radial and toroidal electric fields

A radial electric field produces an electric drift mainly in the poloidal direction when the toroidal magnetic field dominates. It can modify the slower motion of trapped orbits. Its consequences should be assessed in the actual geometry rather than identified with a universal radial particle loss.

An inductive toroidal electric field has another effect: the Ware pinch. In the usual large-aspect-ratio sign convention, a trapped particle has the leading bounce-averaged radial drift

vr,Ware≃-EφBθ.v_{r,\mathrm{Ware}}\simeq-\frac{E_{\varphi}}{B_{\theta}}.

For the conventional current-driving field orientation, this is inward. The signed expression depends on the chosen coordinate and field directions. It describes the trapped population, not the full plasma’s average radial velocity. Passing particles mainly respond by parallel acceleration. See Fitzpatrick’s neoclassical transport derivation.

3.7 Diamagnetic flow and the bootstrap current

A pressure gradient contributes to a species’ perpendicular fluid velocity:

v*s=B×∇psqsnsB2,ps=nskBTs.\mathbf{v}_{*s}=\frac{\mathbf{B}\times\nabla p_s}{q_s n_s B^2},\qquad p_s=n_s k_B T_s.

This diamagnetic velocity is a moment of the particle distribution. In the simple uniform-field example, unequal numbers of gyro-orbits arriving from opposite sides of a pressure gradient give a nonzero local fluid velocity and current, although their guiding centers do not undergo a corresponding drift. It therefore cannot automatically be interpreted as guiding-center transport. The distinction is explained in Drift Equations.

The bootstrap current is a different phenomenon: pressure gradients, trapped particles, and collisional coupling between trapped and passing populations together generate a parallel current in a toroidal plasma. Its calculation requires kinetic or neoclassical theory. Adding single-particle drift velocities is not enough, and the bootstrap current should not be equated with the perpendicular diamagnetic current. Further reading: Parallel Current Density.

01 · 等离子体总览

1.1 从电离气体到集体行为

等离子体包含能够自由运动的带电粒子，通常是电子和离子，也可能同时存在中性原子或分子。电离提供带电粒子；集体行为描述这些粒子如何通过电磁场相互联系。一个扰动可以影响许多粒子，而不只涉及一次局部碰撞中的两个参与者。

这里存在一个反馈过程：粒子运动产生电荷密度和电流密度，它们产生电磁场，电磁场又改变粒子运动。因此，等离子体能够出现屏蔽、振荡和波动等现象。

温度升高通常有利于热电离。在局域热力学平衡下，Saha 关系的示意形式为

neninn∝T3/2exp(-EikBT).\frac{n_e n_i}{n_n}\propto T^{3/2}\exp\left(-\frac{E_i}{k_B T}\right).

其中 nnn_n 为中性粒子密度，EiE_i 为电离能，式中省略了统计因子。在理想 Saha 模型中，温度一定时，提高总密度通常会降低平衡电离比例。实际放电的电离还可能由非热电子、外部辐射和非平衡过程决定，不能一概套用这一平衡关系。

1.2 温度、能量与不同粒子种类

对于各向同性的 Maxwell 分布，每个粒子的平均随机动能为 3kBT/23k_B T/2，对应的热能密度为 3nkBT/23n k_B T/2。因此，稀薄等离子体即使温度很高，其热能密度也未必很大；总能量还取决于体积。

1eV=1.602176634×10-19J,1eVkB≃11605K.1\,\mathrm{eV}=1.602176634\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J},\qquad \frac{1\,\mathrm{eV}}{k_B}\simeq11605\,\mathrm{K}.

电子和离子不一定具有相同温度。如果种类之间的能量交换慢于加热或损失，就可能出现 Te≠TiT_e\ne T_i。在磁化等离子体中，平行与垂直于磁场方向的温度也可能不同。这时，温度是在描述速度分布，不能用一个数概括每一个粒子的状态。

1.3 德拜屏蔽：推导与物理含义

在均匀等离子体中放入一个较弱的正试探电荷 QQ，电子会向它聚集，形成周围的负电荷扰动。试探电荷与周围电荷分布共同作用，使远处的电势减弱。

在一个简单的静态模型中，将单价离子固定在密度 n0n_0，并假设电子温度为 TeT_e、服从 Boltzmann 响应：

ne=n0exp(eφkBTe)≃n0(1+eφkBTe).n_e=n_0\exp\left(\frac{e\phi}{k_B T_e}\right)\simeq n_0\left(1+\frac{e\phi}{k_B T_e}\right).

线性化要求 |eφ|≪kBTe|e\phi|\ll k_B T_e。等离子体产生的感应电荷密度于是为 ρind≃-n0e2φ/(kBTe)\rho_{\mathrm{ind}}\simeq-n_0e^2\phi/(k_B T_e)。将它和试探电荷代入 Poisson 方程，得到

∇2φ-φλDe2=-Qδ(r)ε0,λDe=ε0kBTen0e2.\nabla^2\phi-\frac{\phi}{\lambda_{De}^2}=-\frac{Q\delta(\mathbf{r})}{\epsilon_0},\qquad \lambda_{De}=\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon_0 k_B T_e}{n_0e^2}}.

在球对称条件下，要求无穷远处电势为零，可得

φ(r)=Q4πε0rexp(-rλDe).\phi(r)=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}\exp\left(-\frac{r}{\lambda_{De}}\right).

电子德拜长度 λDe\lambda_{De} 是屏蔽的特征距离。温度越高，电子越难局域聚集；密度越高，可参与屏蔽的电荷越多。因此，λDe∝Te/n0\lambda_{De}\propto\sqrt{T_e/n_0}。

屏蔽是逐渐发生的，电场不会在一个德拜长度处突然消失。强试探电荷附近也可能超出线性近似的适用范围。若多个 Maxwell 分布的粒子种类都能够响应，则有 λD-2=∑snsqs2/(ε0kBTs)\lambda_D^{-2}=\sum_s n_s q_s^2/(\epsilon_0 k_B T_s)。平衡屏蔽模型可进一步参阅 Fitzpatrick 的 Debye Shielding。

1.4 准中性是与尺度有关的近似

在远大于屏蔽长度的尺度上，等离子体主体通常近似满足电中性：

ne≃∑iZini,L≫λD.n_e\simeq\sum_i Z_i n_i,\qquad L\gg\lambda_D.

准中性不意味着电场为零，也不意味着每一个微小体积中的正负电荷严格相等。很小的电荷不平衡也可以维持重要的电场。鞘层和其他德拜尺度结构需要允许电荷分离的描述；非中性等离子体也存在。因此，大尺度准中性模型是一种有用的适用情形，并不是涵盖所有等离子体的定义。

1.5 等离子体振荡与响应时间

设想在冷、均匀的平板模型中，电子流体相对固定的离子整体位移了一个小距离 xx。由此产生的电场提供回复力，电子运动方程成为

d2xdt2+ωpe2x=0,ωpe=nee2ε0me.\frac{d^2x}{dt^2}+\omega_{pe}^2x=0,\qquad \omega_{pe}=\sqrt{\frac{n_e e^2}{\epsilon_0m_e}}.

这给出了电子等离子体角频率。响应的特征时间约为 1/ωpe1/\omega_{pe}，完整振荡周期则为 2π/ωpe2\pi/\omega_{pe}，两者不能混为一谈。电荷为 ZieZ_i e 的离子具有相应的种类频率 ωpi=niZi2e2/(ε0mi)\omega_{pi}=\sqrt{n_i Z_i^2e^2/(\epsilon_0m_i)}；由于质量较大，离子通常响应得更慢。

有限温度和空间变化会引入更丰富的波动物理。不过，这个简单振子已经说明：微小的电荷位移能够产生一个集体回复电场。

1.6 大量粒子与大量小角偏转

对于弱耦合等离子体，一个德拜球内通常包含许多粒子：

ND=4π3neλD3≫1.N_D=\frac{4\pi}{3}n_e\lambda_D^3\gg1.

较大的粒子数使平滑的集体描述有意义。库仑碰撞仍然存在，通常表现为许多小角偏转的累积。在常见弱耦合情形下，对固定粒子种类和电荷态，某一特征库仑碰撞频率的标度为 ν∝nlnΛ/T3/2\nu\propto n\ln\Lambda/T^{3/2}，其中 lnΛ\ln\Lambda 为库仑对数。具体定义和系数取决于讨论的是哪一种弛豫过程。

碰撞是否重要，必须结合研究过程的时间尺度判断。例如，振荡频率远高于相关碰撞频率时，可以近似采用无碰撞描述。在部分电离等离子体中，中性粒子碰撞可能很重要；应当比较其时间尺度与研究过程，而不是使用一条普遍适用的不等式。

自洽电磁描述以 Maxwell 方程为基础：

∇·E=ρε0,∇·B=0.\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}=\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0},\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0.
∇×E=-∂B∂t,∇×B=μ0J+μ0ε0∂E∂t.\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t},\qquad \nabla\times\mathbf{B}=\mu_0\mathbf{J}+\mu_0\epsilon_0\frac{\partial\mathbf{E}}{\partial t}.

复习检查。 区分温度与能量密度；推导屏蔽电势；区分 1/ωpe1/\omega_{pe} 与 2π/ωpe2\pi/\omega_{pe}；判断什么时候可以采用准中性或无碰撞近似。

02 · 单粒子（一）

2.1 选择描述层次

试探粒子模型追踪给定场中的一条轨道；动理学模型描述分布函数 fs(r,v,t)f_s(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{v},t)，同时考虑粒子在速度空间中的分布；流体模型描述密度、整体速度、压强等矩。MHD 是具有特定假设的一类流体近似。

对于经典、非相对论试探粒子，运动方程为

drdt=v,mdvdt=q(E+v×B).\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}=\mathbf{v},\qquad m\frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt}=q(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}).

在自洽动理学计算中，ns=∫fsd3vn_s=\int f_s\,d^3v 和 J=∑sqs∫vfsd3v\mathbf{J}=\sum_s q_s\int\mathbf{v}f_s\,d^3v 将粒子分布与电磁场联系起来。在给定场中研究单条轨道是一个有用起点，但仅凭它还不能确定等离子体平衡或输运系数。

2.2 均匀磁场：回旋运动与平行运动

取 B=Bz^\mathbf{B}=B\hat{\mathbf{z}}、E=0\mathbf{E}=0。定义带符号的频率 Ω=qB/m\Omega=qB/m，以及取正值的回旋频率 ωc=|q|B/m\omega_c=|q|B/m，则

dvxdt=Ωvy,dvydt=-Ωvx,dv∥dt=0.\frac{dv_x}{dt}=\Omega v_y,\qquad \frac{dv_y}{dt}=-\Omega v_x,\qquad \frac{dv_{\parallel}}{dt}=0.

垂直速度的大小不变、方向不断转动，粒子在垂直于磁场的平面中做圆周运动，拉莫尔半径为

ρL=v⊥ωc=mv⊥|q|B.\rho_L=\frac{v_{\perp}}{\omega_c}=\frac{m v_{\perp}}{|q|B}.

叠加平行运动后，轨道成为螺旋线。电荷的正负决定旋转方向。磁力改变运动方向，但不做功，因为 v·(v×B)=0\mathbf{v}\cdot(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})=0。因此，均匀磁场能够限制垂直方向的运动，却不阻止粒子沿磁场运动。

2.3 导引中心及其适用范围

将快速回旋与圆心的缓慢运动分开，可以建立导引中心描述。在最低阶近似下，导引中心沿磁场运动，并叠加垂直方向的漂移。要使这一近似成立，粒子在一次回旋中感受到的场变化必须很小：

ρLLB≪1,1ωcB|dBdt|≪1.\frac{\rho_L}{L_B}\ll1,\qquad \frac{1}{\omega_c B}\left|\frac{dB}{dt}\right|\ll1.

其中 LBL_B 为磁场变化的特征尺度，时间导数沿相关运动路径计算。磁场方向和电场的变化也需要满足相应的缓变条件。靠近磁零点或陡峭梯度时，导引中心近似可能失效。

标量磁矩定义为

μ=mv⊥22B=W⊥B.\mu=\frac{m v_{\perp}^2}{2B}=\frac{W_{\perp}}{B}.

在适当的缓变条件下，它近似守恒。第 3 节将用这一性质理解磁镜反射。

2.4 电漂移与一般力漂移

在均匀静态场中，垂直电场使回旋轨道整体以如下速度平移：

vE=E×BB2.\mathbf{v}_E=\frac{\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}}{B^2}.

令缓慢运动的垂直力平衡为 E⊥+vE×B=0\mathbf{E}_{\perp}+\mathbf{v}_E\times\mathbf{B}=0，即可得到这一表达式。它与粒子电荷和质量无关：同一场中的电子和离子具有相同的电漂移。平行电场则通过 mdv∥/dt=qE∥m\,dv_{\parallel}/dt=qE_{\parallel} 使粒子沿磁场加速。

若单个粒子还受到另一个较弱、缓慢变化的垂直力 F⊥\mathbf{F}_{\perp}，则有

vF=F×BqB2.\mathbf{v}_F=\frac{\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{B}}{qB^2}.

只有力的垂直分量参与这一漂移。代入 F=qE\mathbf{F}=q\mathbf{E}，便恢复电漂移公式，也解释了电荷为什么会抵消。若某个力不随电荷正负而反向，它通常会使正负粒子向相反方向漂移。

2.5 梯度漂移与曲率漂移

磁场强度的梯度使回旋轨道两侧略有不同，对应的有效力为 -μ∇B-\mu\nabla B，因此

v∇B=μqB2B×∇B.\mathbf{v}_{\nabla B}=\frac{\mu}{qB^2}\mathbf{B}\times\nabla B.

曲率漂移来自另一个原因：即使磁场大小局部不变，沿弯曲磁力线运动的粒子也必须不断转向。定义指向局部曲率中心的曲率矢量 κ=(b·∇)b\boldsymbol{\kappa}=(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{b}，则

vcurv=mv∥2qBb×κ.\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{curv}}=\frac{m v_{\parallel}^2}{qB}\mathbf{b}\times\boldsymbol{\kappa}.

两者都与电荷正负有关。梯度漂移取决于垂直能量，曲率漂移取决于平行能量，是可以同时存在的不同贡献。这些最低阶公式要求粒子充分磁化，并满足导引中心近似的尺度条件。Fitzpatrick 的 Magnetic Drifts 给出了漂移项及其几何解释。

在简单环向磁场中，Bφ∝1/RB_{\varphi}\propto1/R，内侧磁场强于外侧。梯度和曲率漂移会使正负电荷在竖直方向分离；由此产生的电场又可能驱动共同的跨环漂移。这说明磁约束不能只依赖纯环向磁场，也为加入极向磁场、形成螺旋磁力线提供了物理动机。

2.6 随时间变化的场：极化漂移与 betatron 效应

若磁场均匀且静止，而 E⊥\mathbf{E}_{\perp} 随时间缓慢变化，导引中心还会出现极化漂移：

vpol=mqB2dE⊥dt.\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{pol}}=\frac{m}{qB^2}\frac{d\mathbf{E}_{\perp}}{dt}.

它反映粒子对变化中的电漂移的惯性响应。与 vE\mathbf{v}_E 不同，极化漂移依赖电荷和质量。空间均匀电场中的导数可以直接采用时间导数；更一般的非均匀情形，需要与导引中心运动一致地计算沿轨道的导数。

变化的磁场会通过 Faraday 定律产生感应电场。如果变化仍然满足绝热条件、μ\mu 保持守恒，则

W⊥=μB,dW⊥dt=μdBdt.W_{\perp}=\mu B,\qquad \frac{dW_{\perp}}{dt}=\mu\frac{dB}{dt}.

因此，增强磁场可以提高垂直动能，这就是 betatron 效应。能量来自感应电场，并不意味着 Lorentz 力中的磁力部分做了功。

2.7 空间非均匀电场与快速振荡电场

如果电场在一个拉莫尔轨道内存在明显变化，粒子一次回旋就会采样不同位置的电场。局部电漂移公式此时只是最低阶项；当 ρL/LE\rho_L/L_E 不再很小时，有限拉莫尔半径修正就需要考虑。

快速振荡电场涉及另一种平均过程。对于非相对论、可以忽略背景磁化效应的粒子，若 E(r,t)=E0(r)cosωt\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r},t)=\mathbf{E}_0(\mathbf{r})\cos\omega t 的振幅空间变化缓慢，且振荡位移较小，则周期平均后的有质动力为

Fpond=-q24mω2∇|E0|2.\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{pond}}=-\frac{q^2}{4m\omega^2}\nabla|\mathbf{E}_0|^2.

这里 E0\mathbf{E}_0 指峰值振幅，不是 RMS 电场。由于出现 q2q^2，力的方向与电荷正负无关，指向场强平方较低的区域。显著磁化或接近共振时，需要另外的描述，不能原样沿用这个公式。可参阅 Motion in Oscillating Fields。

03 · 单粒子（二）

3.1 绝热不变量与分层时间尺度

严格不变量来自运动方程的严格性质，例如对称性。绝热不变量则是在周期运动所处环境变化足够缓慢时近似守恒的量。对于一维周期 Hamilton 系统，作用量为

J=∮pxdx.J=\oint p_x\,dx.

这个积分对应相空间中的一条闭合轨道。其中 pxp_x 是正则动量；对于电磁场中的粒子，p=mv+qA\mathbf{p}=m\mathbf{v}+q\mathbf{A}，不能简单地将它写成 mvm\mathbf{v}。作用量近似守恒时，能量仍然可以变化。Fitzpatrick 的 Adiabatic Invariants 将这一作用量与磁矩联系起来。

对于具有良好尺度分离的俘获粒子，回旋运动最快，反弹运动较慢，漂移或进动更慢：

ωc≫ωb≫ωd.\omega_c\gg\omega_b\gg\omega_d.

第一绝热不变量为 μ=W⊥/B\mu=W_{\perp}/B，第二不变量为反弹作用量。对于闭合的慢漂移轨道，还可以定义第三不变量，并在适当构型下将其近似联系到轨道包围的磁通。要保持较慢运动的作用量，环境就必须相对于那个更长的周期变化得足够慢；仅有 μ\mu 守恒，并不能保证其他作用量也守恒。

磁通解释尤其适用于磁层中的漂移壳，并不意味着每一个托卡马克粒子都严格保持某个任意选取的包围磁通。对于满足假定场对称性的轴对称系统，正则环向动量的守恒更为直接。可参阅 Third Adiabatic Invariant。

3.2 磁镜：磁力不做功，粒子仍会反射

考虑一个静态、平滑变化且没有电场的磁场。在导引中心近似下，粒子总动能 WW 和磁矩 μ\mu 守恒：

W=mv∥22+μB.W=\frac{m v_{\parallel}^2}{2}+\mu B.

当粒子进入更强的磁场时，W⊥=μBW_{\perp}=\mu B 增大，平行动能随之减小。这是在固定总能量内部重新分配动能。沿磁力线求导，可以得到磁镜力：

mdv∥dt=-μ∂B∂s,m\frac{dv_{\parallel}}{dt}=-\mu\frac{\partial B}{\partial s},

其中 ss 为沿磁力线的距离。反射点满足 v∥=0v_{\parallel}=0，即 Bm=W/μB_m=W/\mu。

在磁场大小为 B0B_0 的参考点，定义投掷角 α0\alpha_0 为速度与磁场的夹角，满足 sinα0=v⊥0/v\sin\alpha_0=v_{\perp0}/v。由 μ=Wsin2α0/B0\mu=W\sin^2\alpha_0/B_0 得到

sin2α0=B0Bm.\sin^2\alpha_0=\frac{B_0}{B_m}.

若粒子能够到达的最大磁场为 BmaxB_{\max}，损失锥边界满足 sin2αLC=B0/Bmax\sin^2\alpha_{\mathrm{LC}}=B_0/B_{\max}。sin2α0\sin^2\alpha_0 更小的粒子，在到达最大场强之前不会反射。损失锥是速度空间中围绕平行和反平行方向的区域，并不是实空间中的一个锥形开口。

碰撞可以将俘获粒子散射进损失锥，因此磁镜约束并非完全无损。共振扰动和磁零点也可能破坏磁矩守恒所依赖的条件。Magnetic Mirrors 讨论了俘获与损失锥。回旋、反射和慢漂移之间的关系，也有助于理解地球磁场中俘获粒子的基本运动。

3.3 反弹作用量与缓慢压缩

在不含电场的简单磁镜模型中，粒子被俘获在两个反射点 s1s_1 与 s2s_2 之间，其第二不变量为

J∥=∮mv∥ds=2∫s1s22m[W-μB(s)]ds.J_{\parallel}=\oint m v_{\parallel}\,ds=2\int_{s_1}^{s_2}\sqrt{2m[W-\mu B(s)]}\,ds.

闭合积分沿相空间中的完整往返反弹轨道计算，两段贡献相加，而不是相消。当构型变化远慢于反弹周期、轨道始终保持俘获且不穿越分界轨道时，这一作用量近似守恒。

如果两个反射边界之间的距离缓慢缩短，反弹作用量守恒可以使平行动能增加。对于内部磁场近似均匀的理想模型，J∥≃2m|v∥|LJ_{\parallel}\simeq2m|v_{\parallel}|L 给出 W∥∝L-2W_{\parallel}\propto L^{-2}。这说明了绝热 Fermi 加速的基本思想；真实磁镜剖面需要使用完整积分。可参阅 Second Adiabatic Invariant。

3.4 托卡马克几何与通行、俘获粒子

托卡马克将环向磁场与极向磁场组合成螺旋磁力线。通常由外部线圈提供主要环向场，环向等离子体电流提供相当一部分极向场。仿星器主要依靠外部三维线圈形成约束构型；仅凭这一差别，不能直接判断哪一种装置的约束或稳定性更好。

对于示意性的圆形磁面，柱坐标 (R,φ,Z)(R,\varphi,Z) 与局部坐标 (r,θ,φ)(r,\theta,\varphi) 满足

R=R0+rcosθ,Z=rsinθ.R=R_0+r\cos\theta,\qquad Z=r\sin\theta.

其中 θ=0\theta=0 为外侧中平面。在大环径比、环向场占主导的近似下，

B(θ)≃Bref1+εtcosθ,εt=rR0≪1.B(\theta)\simeq\frac{B_{\mathrm{ref}}}{1+\epsilon_t\cos\theta},\qquad \epsilon_t=\frac{r}{R_0}\ll1.

磁场外侧较弱、内侧较强。忽略碰撞和显著电势变化，粒子的平行速度为

v∥=±2m[W-μB(θ)].v_{\parallel}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{m}[W-\mu B(\theta)]}.

通行粒子具有足够的平行动能，可以经过强场区域，完成一周极向运动。俘获粒子则在到达强场区域前反射，在外侧两个反射点之间往返。是否反射取决于能量与投掷角，而不是电荷正负的变化。

3.5 香蕉轨道是导引中心轨道

磁漂移会使反弹粒子的导引中心偏离单条磁力线，其在极向平面的投影可以形成香蕉形轨道。香蕉轨道不是微观拉莫尔圆；真实粒子的快速回旋仍叠加在导引中心轨道上。

在简单圆形磁面、大环径比托卡马克中，热粒子轨道的特征径向宽度具有以下标度：

Δpass∼qsafρs,Δbanana∼qsafρsεt,\Delta_{\mathrm{pass}}\sim q_{\mathrm{saf}}\rho_s,\qquad \Delta_{\mathrm{banana}}\sim\frac{q_{\mathrm{saf}}\rho_s}{\sqrt{\epsilon_t}},

其中 ρs=vth,s/ωc,s\rho_s=v_{\mathrm{th},s}/\omega_{c,s}，vth,s=2kBTs/msv_{\mathrm{th},s}=\sqrt{2k_B T_s/m_s}。这是量级估算，不是对所有投掷角都成立的精确宽度。靠近通行与俘获的分界，或有限轨道宽度已经不能当作小修正时，应当重新检查适用性。Trapped and Passing Particles 介绍了这一理想化几何与轨道分类。

回旋平均消去快速旋转，得到导引中心描述；反弹平均进一步消去往返运动，用于描述俘获轨道更慢的演化。最低阶反弹平均中的平行运动可以相互抵消，但不能据此认为所有运动都消失了：磁进动和电漂移仍然存在。

3.6 径向电场与环向电场

当环向磁场占主导时，径向电场产生的电漂移主要沿极向方向，能够改变俘获轨道的慢运动。其结果需要结合实际几何判断，不能直接将它等同于普遍的径向粒子损失。

感应环向电场还会产生另一种效应：Ware 内向漂移。在常用的大环径比符号约定下，俘获粒子的最低阶反弹平均径向漂移为

vr,Ware≃-EφBθ.v_{r,\mathrm{Ware}}\simeq-\frac{E_{\varphi}}{B_{\theta}}.

对于通常用于驱动电流的场方向，这一漂移朝内。带符号的表达式依赖所选坐标和场方向。它描述的是俘获粒子群，不是全体等离子体的平均径向速度；通行粒子主要通过平行加速响应。可参阅 Fitzpatrick 的 Neoclassical Transport 推导。

3.7 抗磁流与自举电流

压强梯度对某一种粒子的垂直流体速度贡献为

v*s=B×∇psqsnsB2,ps=nskBTs.\mathbf{v}_{*s}=\frac{\mathbf{B}\times\nabla p_s}{q_s n_s B^2},\qquad p_s=n_s k_B T_s.

抗磁速度是粒子分布的一个矩。在简单均匀磁场例子中，由于压强梯度两侧到达某一点的回旋轨道数量不等，局部流体速度和电流可以不为零，即使导引中心并没有相应的整体漂移。因此，不能自动把抗磁流解释为导引中心输运。Drift Equations 说明了这一差别。

自举电流则是另一个现象：在环形等离子体中，压强梯度、俘获粒子及俘获与通行粒子之间的碰撞耦合共同产生平行电流。计算它需要动理学或新经典理论，仅仅相加单粒子漂移速度还不够，也不能将它与垂直抗磁电流混为一谈。可进一步参阅 Parallel Current Density。
