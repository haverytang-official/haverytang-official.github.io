From pickup-coil voltage to magnetic flux

Leyan TANG · Reading Notes · Strait et al. (2008), II.C.1 — Integrators

A pickup coil responds to changing magnetic flux. An integrator turns that voltage into a signal proportional to the accumulated flux change. The practical challenge is to retain the wanted signal while preventing small electrical errors from accumulating with it.

1. Reading question ①: Why must the signal be integrated?

Status: resolved. The coil voltage represents a rate of change, whereas the quantity we want to reconstruct is magnetic flux, or a magnetic-field component after calibration.

For a fixed coil, define the flux linkage as Ψ=∑k=1NΦk\Psi=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Phi_k. If all turns intercept the same flux, Ψ=NΦ\Psi=N\Phi. Treat VinV_{\mathrm{in}} here as the ideal induced emf, or a terminal signal calibrated to represent it. Faraday’s law, with a chosen terminal polarity, gives

Vin(t)=-dΨdt.V_{\mathrm{in}}(t)=-\frac{d\Psi}{dt}.

Integrating from the start of the measurement t0t_0 yields

Ψ(t)-Ψ(t0)=-∫t0tVin(u)du.\Psi(t)-\Psi(t_0)=-\int_{t_0}^{t}V_{\mathrm{in}}(u)\,du.

For a small, fixed coil in an approximately uniform field, Ψ≃NAB∥\Psi\simeq NA B_{\parallel}, where B∥B_{\parallel} is the field component along the coil axis and AA is the average area per turn. The integrated signal can then be calibrated to the change in that component.

What changed in my understanding: the probe supplies the derivative signal; the integrator supplies a representation of its accumulated change. The integration constant matters. A stationary induction coil cannot determine an unknown static magnetic field merely by integrating a zero voltage; an absolute value requires an independently known initial condition or reference.

2. What is the integrator designed to output?

Write an ideal inverting integrator as

Vout(t)-Vout(t0)=-1τI∫t0tVin(u)du.V_{\mathrm{out}}(t)-V_{\mathrm{out}}(t_0)=-\frac{1}{\tau_I}\int_{t_0}^{t}V_{\mathrm{in}}(u)\,du.

Here τI\tau_I has units of time and sets the conversion scale. Combining this equation with Faraday’s law gives

Vout(t)-Vout(t0)=Ψ(t)-Ψ(t0)τI.V_{\mathrm{out}}(t)-V_{\mathrm{out}}(t_0)=\frac{\Psi(t)-\Psi(t_0)}{\tau_I}.

The two minus signs cancel for this polarity and an inverting circuit. Reversing the coil terminals changes the calibrated sign. A passive circuit can have another sign convention; the essential requirement is the known proportionality to flux change.

The units are consistent: voltage integrated over time has units of volt-seconds, equivalent to webers; dividing by τI\tau_I produces a voltage again. VinV_{\mathrm{in}} and VoutV_{\mathrm{out}} therefore describe different stages of the measurement chain.

3. Passive integration: useful within a frequency band

3.1 RC circuit: take the output across the capacitor

Consider a resistor in series with the input and a capacitor from the output node to ground, with negligible output loading. Its transfer function is

HRC(s)=Vout(s)Vin(s)=11+sRC,τp=RC.H_{RC}(s)=\frac{V_{\mathrm{out}}(s)}{V_{\mathrm{in}}(s)}=\frac{1}{1+sRC},\qquad \tau_p=RC.

Here ss is the Laplace variable; a sinusoidal response is evaluated at s=iωs=i\omega, with angular frequency ω\omega. When ωτp≫1\omega\tau_p\gg1, the circuit approximates an integrator:

HRC(iω)≃1iωτp.H_{RC}(i\omega)\simeq\frac{1}{i\omega\tau_p}.

It is a low-pass circuit being used in its integration range. “High frequency” here means high relative to 1/τp1/\tau_p, while still below other limits from the sensor, cabling, and electronics. It does not mean that arbitrarily high frequencies are usable. The time-domain equation τpdVout/dt+Vout=Vin\tau_p\,dV_{\mathrm{out}}/dt+V_{\mathrm{out}}=V_{\mathrm{in}} also shows why it is only an approximate integrator.

3.2 LR circuit and coil self-integration

For an ideal series inductor and resistor, measuring the voltage across the resistor gives

HLR(s)=RR+sL=11+sτp,τp=LR.H_{LR}(s)=\frac{R}{R+sL}=\frac{1}{1+s\tau_p},\qquad \tau_p=\frac{L}{R}.

When ωL≫R\omega L\gg R, this also approaches 1/(sτp)1/(s\tau_p). In a self-integrating pickup-coil arrangement, the coil’s own inductance and a resistive load can supply this behavior. In that case, the source for this simple model is the induced emf; the measured voltage is across the load. Winding resistance and the load both enter the actual response, so a real coil requires its circuit model and calibration.

3.3 Why longer measurements become difficult

After a flux change has ended, the ideal integrated value should remain constant. In the simple passive circuits, a stored output instead decays approximately as exp(-T/τp)\exp(-T/\tau_p). For a measurement or hold interval T≪τpT\ll\tau_p, the fractional decay is about T/τpT/\tau_p.

Increasing τp\tau_p reduces this droop, but also decreases the integrated output amplitude: in the approximation, its magnitude is proportional to flux change divided by τp\tau_p. This is the tradeoff behind the concern that a longer integration time can make a passive signal harder to measure. TI’s active-integrator design guide illustrates the passive RC response and the use of amplification to obtain a useful signal level.

4. Active integration: separate conversion scale from retention time

An ideal op-amp integrator uses an input resistor RinR_{\mathrm{in}} and a feedback capacitor CfC_f. With voltages measured relative to the reference at the noninverting input, its virtual-ground approximation gives

dVoutdt=-VinRinCf,τI=RinCf.\frac{dV_{\mathrm{out}}}{dt}=-\frac{V_{\mathrm{in}}}{R_{\mathrm{in}}C_f},\qquad \tau_I=R_{\mathrm{in}}C_f.

An ideal circuit retains its output when the input becomes zero, rather than having the same passive decay term. The conversion scale τI\tau_I is therefore not, by itself, the maximum duration of reliable integration. Active circuitry also allows the signal level to be adjusted, subject to noise, bandwidth, and output-voltage limits.

Real designs often place a large resistor RfR_f in parallel with CfC_f to stabilize the DC operating point. For an ideal op amp, this gives

HA(s)=-Rf/Rin1+sRfCf.H_A(s)=-\frac{R_f/R_{\mathrm{in}}}{1+sR_fC_f}.

The low-frequency pole and retention time are then set by τh=RfCf\tau_h=R_fC_f, while the integration scale remains RinCfR_{\mathrm{in}}C_f. Integration requires ωτh≫1\omega\tau_h\gg1 within the amplifier’s usable bandwidth. These are two different RC products. Increasing RinCfR_{\mathrm{in}}C_f still reduces output per unit integrated input; active integration does not remove that algebraic relation. TI’s integrator circuit description explains the operating range and the role of the feedback resistor.

5. Integrator drift: a small offset becomes a growing error

Suppose the integrator receives the true sensor voltage plus a small equivalent input offset:

Vmeas(t)=Vin(t)+εv(t).V_{\mathrm{meas}}(t)=V_{\mathrm{in}}(t)+\epsilon_v(t).

In the ideal integration model, the unwanted contribution is

δVout(t)=-1τI∫t0tεv(u)du.\delta V_{\mathrm{out}}(t)=-\frac{1}{\tau_I}\int_{t_0}^{t}\epsilon_v(u)\,du.

If the offset is approximately constant,

δVout(t)=-εvτI(t-t0).\delta V_{\mathrm{out}}(t)=-\frac{\epsilon_v}{\tau_I}(t-t_0).

For example, a 10μV10\,\mu\mathrm{V} equivalent input offset integrated for 100s100\,\mathrm{s} with τI=0.1s\tau_I=0.1\,\mathrm{s} produces a 10mV10\,\mathrm{mV} output error in magnitude. Extending the measurement to 1000s1000\,\mathrm{s} makes that error 100mV100\,\mathrm{mV} if the same model and offset remain applicable.

An offset is not evidence of a DC plasma current. It may originate in amplifier imperfections, bias currents, thermal voltages, or leakage. Nor is every constant input voltage an error: a real, steady flux ramp also induces a constant voltage. The instrument cannot distinguish those two cases from the voltage alone without a reference or further information.

EffectWhat is happening?Typical simple-model behavior
DroopA stored integrated value leaks awayDecay on a retention timescale
DriftAn unwanted input contribution accumulatesLinear growth for a constant equivalent offset

6. Baseline correction between pulses

One approach is to estimate the offset during a reference interval in which the sensor is known to produce no significant physical signal. An auxiliary servo adjusts the correction, and the resulting value is held during the pulse:

Vcorr(t)=Vmeas(t)-ε^v.V_{\mathrm{corr}}(t)=V_{\mathrm{meas}}(t)-\hat{\epsilon}_v.

The estimate ε^v\hat{\epsilon}_v reduces the residual error, which now depends on εv(t)-ε^v\epsilon_v(t)-\hat{\epsilon}_v. This estimate-and-hold approach is described for magnetic diagnostics in Marconato et al. (2020), Section 3.1.1.

The reference interval must be chosen deliberately: a gap between plasma pulses can still contain a real signal if the magnetic coils are ramping. During measurement, a servo that keeps forcing the output toward zero could erase genuine slowly varying magnetic information. In the estimate-and-hold scheme, the automatic baseline adjustment is paused while its correction remains applied.

The integrator’s main capacitive feedback remains active. Pausing the auxiliary zeroing loop does not mean disconnecting all feedback around the op amp. The correction also remains imperfect if the offset changes after the reference interval. Resetting the initial output and correcting an input offset are related operations, but resetting alone does not prevent a continuing offset from accumulating.

7. My understanding of the measurement chain

  • The pickup coil supplies a voltage proportional to the time derivative of flux linkage.
  • Integration and calibration convert that voltage into a representation of flux change.
  • Passive RC or LR arrangements integrate approximately over a limited frequency range; increasing retention time reduces droop but weakens the output.
  • Active circuits allow more flexibility in conversion scale and retention, while introducing offset and drift concerns.
  • Reliable long measurements require both a useful signal level and control of accumulated errors.

The central lesson is that an integrator does more than apply a mathematical integral: its calibration, initial condition, bandwidth, and baseline stability all determine whether the reconstructed magnetic signal can be trusted.

从拾取线圈电压到磁通

Leyan TANG · 文献阅读笔记 · Strait 等（2008），Ⅱ.C.1 — 积分器

拾取线圈响应变化的磁通，积分器则把它产生的电压转换为与累积磁通变化成比例的信号。实际测量的难点，是保留需要的信号，同时避免微小的电路误差一起累积。

1. 阅读疑问①：为什么必须对信号积分？

状态：已解决。 线圈电压表示变化率，而我们想重建的量是磁通，或者经过标定后的某个磁场分量。

对于固定线圈，定义磁链为 Ψ=∑k=1NΦk\Psi=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Phi_k。若每一匝截获的磁通相同，则 Ψ=NΦ\Psi=N\Phi。这里将 VinV_{\mathrm{in}} 视为理想感应电动势，或经过标定、能够代表它的端子信号。在选定端子极性的约定下，Faraday 定律给出

Vin(t)=-dΨdt.V_{\mathrm{in}}(t)=-\frac{d\Psi}{dt}.

从测量起始时刻 t0t_0 积分，有

Ψ(t)-Ψ(t0)=-∫t0tVin(u)du.\Psi(t)-\Psi(t_0)=-\int_{t_0}^{t}V_{\mathrm{in}}(u)\,du.

对于处于近似均匀磁场中的固定小线圈，Ψ≃NAB∥\Psi\simeq NA B_{\parallel}，其中 B∥B_{\parallel} 是沿线圈轴的磁场分量，AA 是每匝平均面积。因此，积分信号可以标定为这一分量的变化。

我的理解如何变化： 探针提供的是导数信号，积分器提供的是累积变化的表示。积分常数不能忽略。固定的感应线圈不能仅仅通过对零电压积分来确定一个未知的静态磁场；要得到绝对值，还需要独立已知的初始条件或参考值。

2. 积分器的输出是怎样定义的？

一个理想反相积分器可以写为

Vout(t)-Vout(t0)=-1τI∫t0tVin(u)du.V_{\mathrm{out}}(t)-V_{\mathrm{out}}(t_0)=-\frac{1}{\tau_I}\int_{t_0}^{t}V_{\mathrm{in}}(u)\,du.

其中 τI\tau_I 的单位是时间，用于确定转换比例。与 Faraday 定律结合，得到

Vout(t)-Vout(t0)=Ψ(t)-Ψ(t0)τI.V_{\mathrm{out}}(t)-V_{\mathrm{out}}(t_0)=\frac{\Psi(t)-\Psi(t_0)}{\tau_I}.

对于这一端子极性和反相电路，两个负号抵消。交换线圈端子会改变标定后的符号；被动电路也可能对应另一种符号约定。关键是输出与磁通变化之间具有已知的比例关系。

量纲也一致：电压对时间积分的单位是伏秒，等价于韦伯；再除以 τI\tau_I，就得到电压。因此，VinV_{\mathrm{in}} 与 VoutV_{\mathrm{out}} 描述的是测量链中不同阶段的信号。

3. 被动积分：只在一定频段内成立

3.1 RC 电路：在电容两端取输出

考虑一个输入端串联电阻、输出节点通过电容接地的电路，并忽略输出负载。其传递函数为

HRC(s)=Vout(s)Vin(s)=11+sRC,τp=RC.H_{RC}(s)=\frac{V_{\mathrm{out}}(s)}{V_{\mathrm{in}}(s)}=\frac{1}{1+sRC},\qquad \tau_p=RC.

其中 ss 为 Laplace 变量；正弦响应取 s=iωs=i\omega，ω\omega 为角频率。当 ωτp≫1\omega\tau_p\gg1 时，电路近似为积分器：

HRC(iω)≃1iωτp.H_{RC}(i\omega)\simeq\frac{1}{i\omega\tau_p}.

这实际上是在低通电路的积分频段内使用它。这里的“高频”是相对于 1/τp1/\tau_p 而言，同时仍需低于传感器、线缆和电子学的其他限制，并不是频率越高越好。时域方程 τpdVout/dt+Vout=Vin\tau_p\,dV_{\mathrm{out}}/dt+V_{\mathrm{out}}=V_{\mathrm{in}} 也说明了它为什么只是近似积分器。

3.2 LR 电路与线圈自积分

对于理想串联电感和电阻，在电阻两端测量电压，可得

HLR(s)=RR+sL=11+sτp,τp=LR.H_{LR}(s)=\frac{R}{R+sL}=\frac{1}{1+s\tau_p},\qquad \tau_p=\frac{L}{R}.

当 ωL≫R\omega L\gg R 时，它同样趋近于 1/(sτp)1/(s\tau_p)。在自积分拾取线圈中，线圈本身的电感和电阻负载可以提供这种响应。此时，简单模型的源是感应电动势，实际测量的是负载两端电压。绕组电阻和负载都会影响真实响应，因此需要具体电路模型与标定。

3.3 为什么长时间测量变得困难？

当磁通变化结束后，理想积分值应当保持不变；但在简单被动电路中，存储的输出会近似按 exp(-T/τp)\exp(-T/\tau_p) 衰减。对于测量或保持时段 T≪τpT\ll\tau_p，相对衰减约为 T/τpT/\tau_p。

增大 τp\tau_p 能减小这种衰减，却也会减小积分输出的幅值：在积分近似下，幅值正比于磁通变化除以 τp\tau_p。这就是“延长积分时间可能使被动信号更难测量”的权衡。TI 的主动积分器设计指南 展示了被动 RC 响应，以及如何通过放大得到可用的信号幅值。

4. 主动积分：区分转换比例与保持时间

理想运放积分器采用输入电阻 RinR_{\mathrm{in}} 和反馈电容 CfC_f。当电压都相对于同相端的参考电位表示时，虚地近似给出

dVoutdt=-VinRinCf,τI=RinCf.\frac{dV_{\mathrm{out}}}{dt}=-\frac{V_{\mathrm{in}}}{R_{\mathrm{in}}C_f},\qquad \tau_I=R_{\mathrm{in}}C_f.

理想电路在输入变为零后能够保持输出，而不像上述被动电路那样含有相同的衰减项。因此，转换比例 τI\tau_I 本身并不是可靠积分时间的上限。主动电路也能调节信号幅值，但受到噪声、带宽和输出电压范围的约束。

实际设计常在 CfC_f 两端并联一个较大的电阻 RfR_f，以稳定直流工作点。对于理想运放，这时有

HA(s)=-Rf/Rin1+sRfCf.H_A(s)=-\frac{R_f/R_{\mathrm{in}}}{1+sR_fC_f}.

低频极点和保持时间由 τh=RfCf\tau_h=R_fC_f 决定，积分转换比例仍为 RinCfR_{\mathrm{in}}C_f。要在运放可用带宽内近似积分，需满足 ωτh≫1\omega\tau_h\gg1。这两个 RC 乘积不是同一个量。增大 RinCfR_{\mathrm{in}}C_f 仍然会减小单位输入积分对应的输出；主动积分并不会消除这一代数关系。TI 的 Integrator circuit 说明了工作频段和反馈电阻的作用。

5. 积分器漂移：小偏置变成不断增长的误差

设积分器接收到真实传感器电压与一个较小的等效输入偏置之和：

Vmeas(t)=Vin(t)+εv(t).V_{\mathrm{meas}}(t)=V_{\mathrm{in}}(t)+\epsilon_v(t).

在理想积分模型中，不需要的输出贡献为

δVout(t)=-1τI∫t0tεv(u)du.\delta V_{\mathrm{out}}(t)=-\frac{1}{\tau_I}\int_{t_0}^{t}\epsilon_v(u)\,du.

若偏置近似不变，则

δVout(t)=-εvτI(t-t0).\delta V_{\mathrm{out}}(t)=-\frac{\epsilon_v}{\tau_I}(t-t_0).

例如，10μV10\,\mu\mathrm{V} 的等效输入偏置，在 τI=0.1s\tau_I=0.1\,\mathrm{s} 的条件下积分 100s100\,\mathrm{s}，会产生幅值为 10mV10\,\mathrm{mV} 的输出误差。如果同一模型和偏置仍然适用，将测量延长到 1000s1000\,\mathrm{s}，误差就达到 100mV100\,\mathrm{mV}。

偏置并不能证明存在直流等离子体电流，它可能来自运放不理想性、偏置电流、热电势或漏电。反过来，也不能把所有恒定输入电压都当作误差：真实磁通以恒定速率变化时，同样会产生恒定感应电压。没有参考或其他信息，仪器仅凭这个电压不能区分两者。

现象发生了什么？简单模型中的典型行为
输出衰减（droop）已存储的积分值逐渐泄漏按保持时间尺度衰减
积分漂移（drift）不需要的输入贡献被累积恒定等效偏置导致线性增长

6. 脉冲间的基线修正

一种方法是在已知传感器没有显著真实信号的参考时段估计偏置：由辅助伺服环路调节修正量，并在脉冲期间保持这个值：

Vcorr(t)=Vmeas(t)-ε^v.V_{\mathrm{corr}}(t)=V_{\mathrm{meas}}(t)-\hat{\epsilon}_v.

估计值 ε^v\hat{\epsilon}_v 可以降低残余误差；此时误差取决于 εv(t)-ε^v\epsilon_v(t)-\hat{\epsilon}_v。这种先估计、再保持的磁诊断方案见 Marconato 等（2020），第 3.1.1 节。

参考时段必须慎重选择：即使处于等离子体脉冲之间，磁体线圈的电流若仍在变化，传感器也可能存在真实信号。测量期间，持续把输出拉回零的伺服环路可能抹去真实的慢变磁信息。在这一估计并保持的方案中，自动基线调节暂停，但修正值仍然施加于信号。

积分器本身的电容反馈仍然工作。 暂停辅助调零环路，不等于断开运放的全部反馈。若参考时段之后偏置发生变化，修正也不会完全准确。复位初始输出和修正输入偏置是相关但不同的操作；单纯复位不能防止持续存在的偏置再次累积。

7. 我对测量链的理解

  • 拾取线圈提供与磁链时间导数成比例的电压。
  • 积分与标定把这一电压转换为磁通变化的表示。
  • 被动 RC 或 LR 电路只在有限频段内近似积分；延长保持时间可以减少衰减，却会减弱输出。
  • 主动电路让转换比例和保持能力具有更大的调节空间，同时需要处理偏置和漂移。
  • 可靠的长时间测量，既需要可用的信号幅值，也需要控制累积误差。

这里的核心认识是：积分器不仅是在执行一个数学积分。标定、初始条件、带宽和基线稳定性，共同决定重建的磁信号是否可靠。
